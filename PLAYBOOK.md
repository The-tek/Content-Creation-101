# The Personal Brand Playbook

Every step from the guide, in the order you build a brand. Each step has a goal, questions to answer, rules, mistakes and the lessons it comes from

- Part A: answer these once, in order, before you worry about growing. Come back to them every 3 months
- Part B: run every post through these 4 steps
- Part C: do these every week and every month

## A. Build the brand

Answer these once, in order. Come back to them every 3 months

### Step 1: Decide where you're going

Goal: Know what your brand is for before you post, so you can judge every post with one question: does this get me closer to what I want?

Questions to answer:

1. what do you want this brand to bring you in 3 years? (clients, a job, a product, brand deals or an audience)
2. what would people have to know you for to get that?
3. what do you have to do, not just talk about, to be known for that?
4. what do you need to learn first?
5. at Point A (where your reader is now), what situation do they want to get out of?
6. at Point B (where they want to be), where do you help them get to?
7. how will attention turn into money: brand deals or your own product or service?

Rules:

- start from the end and work backwards: the result, what you're known for, what you do, what you learn
- aim to be known and paid for the right thing
- every post should sit on the path from Point A to Point B
- test every action: does it get me closer to the result?

Mistakes:

- posting before you know what you want to be known for
- judging success by followers when you want clients

Done when: You have 4 lines (result, known for, do, learn) and one sentence that goes from Point A to Point B

Comes from: lesson 1.1 (Why personal brands work so well now); lesson 1.2 (Start from the end: what you want to be known for)

### Step 2: Check your proof

Goal: Know what you can honestly stand behind, so you never have to fake anything

Questions to answer:

1. have you already done what your audience wants to do? Then teach as an expert. Still on the way? Then teach as a student sharing the journey
2. what are 5 real wins, failures or results you have?
3. what could someone else learn from each one?
4. which proof can you show for your main claim?
5. what real weakness or mistake can you mention after the proof?

Rules:

- do the thing first, then talk about it
- pick the route that is true. Posing as an expert when you're a student is lying
- the strongest proof is a result you got for someone like your reader
- give proof first, then a real flaw
- keep adding proof over time

Mistakes:

- teaching what you haven't done
- making up a weakness to look relatable

Done when: You have a list of 5 true lines (wins, failures or results), each with a lesson someone can take from it

Comes from: lesson 1.3 (Proof first: do the thing, then talk about it); lesson 4.10 (Trust, weakness and proof)

### Step 3: Pick the one person you help

Goal: Know exactly who you're talking to, so every post feels written for them

Questions to answer:

1. who is the one person you want to reach?
2. what painful problem do they have that you can solve?
3. what do they want in the next 90 days?
4. where do they want to be in 5 to 10 years?
5. what scares them, and what belief stops them from acting?
6. which exact words do they use for their problem?
7. where do they already spend time online?

Rules:

- write for one specific reader, with one idea and one takeaway per post
- use the exact words your audience uses
- name their painful problem, then solve one problem per piece, again and again
- collect unanswered questions from the comments of big posts in your niche (your small topic area)

Mistakes:

- writing for everyone
- guessing their problems instead of reading their words

Done when: You have a short profile of your person (hopes, dreams, fears, blockers) and 5 of their problems written in their own words

Comes from: lesson 4.8 (Emotion: what drives clicks, shares and action); lesson 1.12 (Trust: how it is built and how to protect it); lesson 11.6 (Use the words your audience already uses); lesson 5.3 (Where good ideas come from)

### Step 4: Choose your niche

Goal: Pick a small topic area you can own, then make it wider as your proof grows

Questions to answer:

1. what's the smallest version of your topic?
2. what have you done that proves you can win there?
3. could you work on this topic for 10 years?
4. could you make 100 or more posts about it?
5. how many followers do accounts in this niche usually have?
6. would it interest core fans, casual viewers and new people?
7. do you have fewer than 30 posts? Then make 20 to 30 on any topic first, then decide

Rules:

- start in the small area where your experience can win
- move to a wider area only when your proof grows, not when you're bored
- own one category before you try five
- every idea should work for experts and for beginners

Mistakes:

- going broad on day one
- switching niches because you're bored instead of because of results

Done when: You have your niche drawn as a middle with rings, and an honest answer to "could I do this for 10 years?"

Comes from: lesson 1.5 (Niche: start narrow, then widen); lesson 1.6 (Size your niche: core, casual and new viewers); lesson 1.13 (Obsession, stickiness and fans); lesson 8.14 (Starting from zero: niche, bio, voice and category)

### Step 5: Position yourself

Goal: Make it clear in one sentence what you do, who it's for, and why people should pick you

Questions to answer:

1. fill in: I help [who] with [what] in [what way]
2. what do you stand for, and why can't people get it anywhere else?
3. which feeling should people have when they see your posts?
4. what do you have that others don't: experience, access, results, a different view or good taste?
5. which 5 things do you want people to link with you?
6. which 3 things do you refuse to be linked with?

Rules:

- a brand is a link in people's heads, built by repeating the same pair of things, like your name and "hooks"
- pair yourself with your 5 links in every piece
- look for the gap just under a popular topic, where existing content stops being useful
- keep your message the same for months

Mistakes:

- taking a position many others could claim too
- changing your message every month

Done when: You have one sentence about what you do, 5 things people link with you, 3 things you refuse to be linked with, and a list of 10 pieces of original work

Comes from: lesson 1.4 (What a brand really is: a link in people's minds); lesson 1.7 (Positioning: promise, proof, perspective, personality); lesson 1.9 (Finding your gap and your advantage)

### Step 6: Build your point of view

Goal: Give people something to agree with, so they remember you

Questions to answer:

1. what 5 things do people in your niche say that you disagree with?
2. what do you believe instead?
3. which belief or habit does your content stand against?
4. what do you want people to believe before they see your offer?
5. what do you teach that deserves a short name?

Rules:

- keep 3 to 5 beliefs that go against the usual advice, and repeat them
- the enemy is a belief or a habit, never a person
- keep pushing the opinion people react to most
- build a world around your brand: named ideas and your own language

Mistakes:

- strong opinions you don't actually believe
- opinions everyone in your niche already says

Done when: You have a list of 3 to 5 beliefs and at least 1 named idea

Comes from: lesson 1.8 (Contrarian takes and a point of view); lesson 1.11 (Worldbuilding: a world your audience lives in); lesson 4.7 (Making old ideas feel new); lesson 4.11 (Identity, sharing, common enemies and disagreement)

### Step 7: Show the person behind it

Goal: Let people get to know you, because people follow people and not ideas

Questions to answer:

1. where were you, what made you change, and where are you now?
2. what 3 beliefs about your topic do you hold strongly?
3. which 5 surprising true facts about your life could start a story?
4. what loss can you share, and what can someone learn from it?
5. what stays private?

Rules:

- your story makes the same idea feel different when you say it
- a hard-time story builds trust only when the reader gets a takeaway
- share real weaknesses only
- keep a story bank: title, point and where to use it

Mistakes:

- only posting wins, which starts to feel fake
- sharing personal details with no takeaway

Done when: You have a 3-line brand story, 5 surprising facts, 1 loss with a takeaway and a list of what stays private

Comes from: lesson 1.10 (Being real, your story and who you are); lesson 3.12 (Finding, writing and editing your stories); lesson 3.13 (Your brand as a story); lesson 3.14 (Trust through transformation, losses and takeaways)

### Step 8: Plan your content

Goal: Turn your brand into a map of what to post, so you never start from zero

Questions to answer:

1. which 2 to 3 broad topics will you cover?
2. which 3 to 5 smaller topics sit under each?
3. which 3 to 4 pillars (main themes) fit your one sentence about what you do?
4. what is each pillar's job: reach, trust or sale?
5. which 1 to 3 formats will you use?
6. how will you split your posts between what worked, new versions and experiments?

Rules:

- pick 3 to 4 topics and 1 to 3 formats, and combine them into formulas you can repeat
- keep the structure, swap the idea
- give every post one job: reach, trust or sale
- aim for about 70% what worked, 20% new versions and 10% experiments

Mistakes:

- too many formats at once
- pillars with no job

Done when: You have a content map, 3 to 4 pillars with a job each, and 1 to 3 formulas

Comes from: lesson 6.1 (Choosing your formats); lesson 1.9 (Finding your gap and your advantage); lesson 10.9 (The funnel: from stranger to buyer); lesson 5.17 (Doing more of what works, and testing new things)

### Step 9: Set up your platform, pace and profile

Goal: Win on one platform first, at a pace you can keep for a year

Questions to answer:

1. where does your person already spend time?
2. are you best at writing, talking or being on camera?
3. how many hours a week do you really have?
4. which one platform will you focus on for 6 months?
5. what pace could you keep for 6 months?
6. what does your bio say: who you help, with what, plus 1 line of proof?
7. where will you collect emails?

Rules:

- master one platform first, then add a second
- first get quality to 7 out of 10, then post more, then push quality to 9
- leave 2 to 3 hours between posts
- start an email list on day one, because a platform can change its rules or ban you

Mistakes:

- spreading across 4 platforms before winning on one
- a bio that says what you are instead of who you help

Done when: You have one platform, one main format, a weekly pace, a bio and a place to collect emails

Comes from: lesson 8.5 (Choosing your platforms); lesson 8.6 (How often and when to post); lesson 6.12 (How often to post: volume, quality and batching); lesson 8.12 (Own your audience: connect your channels); lesson 8.14 (Starting from zero: niche, bio, voice and category)

## B. Make every piece

Run every post through these 4 steps

### Step 10: Pick the idea

Goal: You can't fix a weak topic by editing, so the idea gets most of your time

Questions to answer:

1. do enough people care about the topic?
2. does your audience come to you for it?
3. do you care enough about it?
4. what's the topic, the angle and the format?
5. will it work for core fans, casual viewers and new people?
6. has it worked before, for you or someone else?
7. what does the reader win or lose?

Rules:

- the topic is the most important choice
- 1 topic x 3 angles x 3 formats = 9 ideas
- copy the format, use your own words
- write 20 or more ideas, throw most away

Mistakes:

- making the first idea you had
- copying someone's content instead of their format

Done when: Your idea is written as topic + angle + format, with its job

Comes from: lesson 5.2 (Topic is king); lesson 5.4 (What an idea is made of: topic, angle, format); lesson 5.7 (Finding outliers); lesson 5.9 (Choosing which ideas to make); lesson 5.19 (One big video instead of 10 normal ones, and the 3 stages of packaging)

### Step 11: Package it and write the hook

Goal: Packaging (the title, image and first line) decides whether anyone sees the idea, so it gets the next most time

Questions to answer:

1. can a stranger tell what it's about from the first line alone?
2. why should they care right now?
3. does it start with the benefit or the pain?
4. could the hook be read two ways?
5. did you write 5 to 20 versions and pick the best?
6. do the title, image and first line say the same thing?

Rules:

- say what it's about in the first sentence, in as few words as possible
- start with the problem, add a "but", then end with a clear promise you can deliver
- write the hook last
- don't keep the first line the AI gives you

Mistakes:

- warm-up lines before the point
- hooks that confuse or don't make people care

Done when: Your first line makes sense to a stranger in one second

Comes from: lesson 2.1 (What a hook does and why it decides everything); lesson 2.2 (The master formula: lean, stun, snap back); lesson 2.7 (Hook mistakes 1 and 2: delay and confusion); lesson 2.14 (Hooks in writing: first lines for posts, threads and articles); lesson 5.10 (Why packaging decides the result)

### Step 12: Write it and keep the promise

Goal: Give what the hook promised, in words anyone can follow

Questions to answer:

1. do the first lines after the hook repeat the promise and give a first small win?
2. is every line simple enough for anyone to understand?
3. can you delete any word without changing the meaning?
4. did you use numbers, names and timeframes instead of "a few"?
5. does every tip say what the reader gets from it?
6. did you give everything the hook promised?

Rules:

- keep your ideas smart and your words simple
- write like you talk
- show proof before you teach
- add text boxes people can screenshot

Mistakes:

- a hook the body doesn't deliver on
- vague words like "stuff" or "things" where a real example should be

Done when: Every line earns its place and the promise is kept

Comes from: lesson 2.10 (After the hook: keep the promise, give the answer at the right time); lesson 11.2 (Clear over clever: smart ideas, simple words); lesson 11.4 (Every word earns its place); lesson 11.5 (Be specific: numbers, names and proof); lesson 3.2 (Context plus conflict); lesson 6.16 (Viewer satisfaction: give what the title and thumbnail promised)

### Step 13: Check it before you post

Goal: Catch what would hurt trust or stop people sharing before the post goes out

Questions to answer:

1. would people share it?
2. do you believe everything you're saying?
3. is every claim, number and story true?
4. what is this post's one job: reach, trust or sale?
5. would someone save it?
6. could anything in it make someone mute, block or report you?

Rules:

- people share what says something good about them
- treat a fast-spreading stat as a claim until you find the original source
- if you sell, aim for trust, not millions of views
- don't invent urgency

Mistakes:

- repeating a shaky stat because everyone else does
- a post with no clear job

Done when: It's true, it has one job, and you'd be proud to put your name on it

Comes from: lesson 4.11 (Identity, sharing, common enemies and disagreement); lesson 4.15 (How X picks what to show: one creator's reading of the algorithm); lesson 4.2 (The attention span myth)

## C. Grow, earn and keep going

Every week and every month

### Step 14: Get eyes on it

Goal: Posting alone is not growth. Put your work in front of people who don't know you yet

Questions to answer:

1. where will you put this post in front of new people this week?
2. which bigger accounts can you reply to with a real point of view?
3. whose ideas did you use, and did you credit them?
4. which past winner can you repost or reshape?
5. who could you team up with?

Rules:

- reply and quote to add your own view, not just "great post"
- credit the people whose ideas you use
- every month, rewrite your winners from new angles
- add long pieces and new platforms only with your best ideas

Mistakes:

- posting and walking away
- empty replies that add nothing

Done when: Every post has a plan to reach new people, not just your followers

Comes from: lesson 8.1 (The one rule of growth: get people to see your work); lesson 8.7 (Replies, comments and networking); lesson 8.8 (Borrowing reach: quotes, remixes, collabs and group chats); lesson 8.9 (Reusing content: the waterfall versus the lake); lesson 8.10 (Do more of what works)

### Step 15: Measure and learn

Goal: Turn your results into instructions for the next post

Questions to answer:

1. for your last 20 posts, how many views, follows, leads (people who show interest in buying) and sales did each bring?
2. what do your top 5 posts share that your bottom 5 don't?
3. for your best post, where did people leave?
4. which format is worth keeping?
5. what will you do more of, and less of, next month?

Rules:

- views are an early sign, while follows, leads and money are the real goal
- check your numbers often, but change things monthly, not daily
- judge a format only after enough tries
- treat advice about how the algorithm (the system that decides what people see) works as unproven, and test it yourself

Mistakes:

- judging by views alone
- changing everything after one bad day

Done when: You have 3 instructions for next month, taken from your own best posts

Comes from: lesson 9.5 (Measuring what counts); lesson 9.6 (How often to review: learning from your best posts); lesson 5.17 (Doing more of what works, and testing new things); lesson 1.15 (Plans, timelines and staying power)

### Step 16: Turn trust into money

Goal: Make it easy for the right people to pay you once they trust you

Questions to answer:

1. what do you sell, or what will you sell?
2. fill in: helping [who] go from [where they're stuck] to [where they want to be]
3. is the result worth far more than the price?
4. what's the next step in your content: a free resource, a sales page or a call?
5. how can someone say they're interested: a questionnaire, a DM or a reply?

Rules:

- give first, sell later, and keep the ask smaller than what you gave
- give away your best ideas and sell the help with using them
- with a small audience, sell by hand: posts, DMs and a link to pay
- test an offer before you build it, for example with a poll or a waitlist

Mistakes:

- selling before people trust you
- inventing urgency

Done when: You have one clear promise, one next step in your content and a way for people to say they're interested

Comes from: lesson 10.3 (Give first, sell later); lesson 10.4 (Give away your best ideas, sell the help with using them); lesson 10.5 (Your first product: small, fast, tested); lesson 10.7 (Getting your first client by hand); lesson 10.10 (Building an offer from zero, and a 90-day plan); lesson 8.16 (From views to money: the attention conversion funnel)

### Step 17: Keep going for the long run

Goal: Most brands fail because the person stops, so plan for the long run from day one

Questions to answer:

1. what's your plan for the next 60 days: topics, formats, posts per week and a review date?
2. what will you do when a whole month of posts does badly?
3. what would make you quit, and how will you stop that?
4. where do you keep your ideas and stories?
5. which day of the week is your content day?
6. which steps could AI speed up, and where do you keep the final say?

Rules:

- expect it to take months to get real results
- test widely in the first 60 days, then keep only formats that do well and you enjoy
- fix your process before you blame your creativity
- let AI handle the speed and keep the final say for yourself

Mistakes:

- quitting before the effort adds up
- automating a process that doesn't work yet

Done when: You have a 60-day plan with a review date, one place for ideas and a weekly content day

Comes from: lesson 1.15 (Plans, timelines and staying power); lesson 8.13 (Patience, practice and not quitting); lesson 9.2 (The content workflow loop); lesson 9.3 (Idea banks and swipe files); lesson 9.7 (Simplify before you automate); lesson 9.8 (AI as a helper: speed from AI, judgment from you)
