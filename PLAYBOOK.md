## A. Build the brand

*Answer these once, in order. Come back to them every 3 months*

<a id="s1"></a>

### Decide where you're going

> **Goal:** Know what your brand is for before you post, so you can judge every post with one question: does this get me closer to what I want?

**Answer these**

1. what do you want this brand to bring you in 3 years? (clients, a job, a product, brand deals or an audience)
2. what would people have to know you for to get that?
3. what do you have to actually do, not just talk about, to be known for that?
4. what do you need to learn first?
5. at Point A (where your reader is now), in one sentence, what situation does your reader want to get out of?
6. at Point B (where they want to be), in one sentence, where do you help them get to?
7. does your buyer need to be taught how the solution works (teaching content), or do they mostly need to feel something (fun content)?
8. how will attention turn into money: brand deals (companies pay you to reach your audience) or selling your own product or service?

**Rules from the sources**

- start from the end and work backwards: the result you want, what you need to be known for, what you need to do, and what you need to learn
- aim to be known and paid for the right thing. A big following that earns you little is a trap
- every idea and every post should be somewhere on the path from Point A to Point B
- test every action by asking: does this get me closer to the result or further away?

**Mistakes to avoid**

- posting before you know what you want to be known for
- judging success by followers when what you really want is clients

**You are done when:** You have 4 lines (result, known for, do, learn) and one sentence that goes from Point A to Point B

**Read more:** [1.1 Why personal brands work so well now](guide/01-personal-brand-and-positioning.md#m1-l1) · [1.2 Start from the end: what you want to be known for](guide/01-personal-brand-and-positioning.md#m1-l2)

<a id="s2"></a>

### Check your proof

> **Goal:** Know what you can honestly stand behind, so you never have to fake anything

**Answer these**

1. have you already done what your audience wants to do? Then you can teach as an expert. Are you still on the way? Then you teach as a student who shares the journey. Which one is true for you?
2. what are 5 real wins, failures or results you have, and what could someone else learn from each?
3. if you're a student: what are you working toward right now, how, and what are you doing differently from others?
4. could friends quiz you on each of your topics for 30 minutes with no preparation, and you'd handle it?
5. which proof can you show for your main claim: a result for someone like them, your own result, a big following or a known person saying good things about you, "I was where you are", or something you have in common?
6. after the proof, what real weakness or mistake can you mention?

**Rules from the sources**

- do the thing first, then talk about it. People find out when you fake expertise
- pick the route that is true. Posing as an expert when you're a student is lying
- the strongest proof is a result you got for someone like the viewer. The weakest is a polished setup that only looks impressive
- give proof first, then a real flaw. That order builds trust
- keep adding proof over time: write down what you do, do more, then teach what you did

**Mistakes to avoid**

- teaching what you haven't done
- making up a weakness just to look relatable

**You are done when:** You have a list of 5 true lines (wins, failures or results), each with a lesson someone can take from it

**Read more:** [1.3 Proof first: do the thing, then talk about it](guide/01-personal-brand-and-positioning.md#m1-l3) · [4.10 Trust, weakness and proof](guide/04-psychology-and-attention.md#m4-l10)

<a id="s3"></a>

### Pick the one person you help

> **Goal:** Know exactly who you're talking to, so every post feels written for them

**Answer these**

1. who is the one person you want to reach? (what they do and what stage they're at)
2. what painful problem do they have that you can solve?
3. hopes: what do they want in the next 90 days?
4. dreams: where do they want to be in 5 to 10 years?
5. fears: what scares them?
6. blockers: which belief stops them from acting?
7. which exact words do they use for their problem? (copy them from comments and replies)
8. where do they already spend time online?

**Rules from the sources**

- write for one specific reader, with one idea and one takeaway (the one useful thing the reader leaves with) per post
- use the exact words your audience uses, so they feel understood
- build trust in three steps: name their painful problem, promise to solve one problem per piece, then solve it with clear steps, again and again
- collect questions nobody answered in the comments of big posts in your niche (your small topic area) into a list of problems, in their exact words

**Mistakes to avoid**

- writing for everyone
- guessing their problems instead of reading their words

**You are done when:** You have a short profile of your person (hopes, dreams, fears, blockers) and 5 of their problems written in their own words

**Read more:** [4.8 Emotion: what drives clicks, shares and action](guide/04-psychology-and-attention.md#m4-l8) · [1.12 Trust: how it is built and how to protect it](guide/01-personal-brand-and-positioning.md#m1-l12) · [11.6 Use the words your audience already uses](guide/11-writing-and-copywriting.md#m11-l6) · [5.3 Where good ideas come from](guide/05-ideas-packaging-and-virality.md#m5-l3)

<a id="s4"></a>

### Choose your niche

> **Goal:** Pick a small topic area you can own, then make it wider as your proof grows

**Answer these**

1. what's the smallest version of your topic? Write it in the middle of a page, then draw 2 wider rings around it
2. what have you done that proves you can win in the middle ring?
3. could you spend 40 hours a week on this topic for the next 10 years? If not, which part of it could you stay excited about?
4. could you make 100 or more posts about it?
5. how big is the market? How many followers do the accounts that focus only on this niche usually have?
6. would your ideas interest three kinds of people: core fans (know the topic well), casual viewers (know a bit) and new people (know nothing)?
7. are you already making money from this, or do you have an offer? Then that's your topic. Have you made fewer than about 30 posts? Make 20 to 30 on any topic first, then decide

**Rules from the sources**

- start in the small area where your experience can win. Move to a wider area only when your proof grows, and not because you're bored
- own one category before you try five
- check how big the market is before you blame the niche
- every idea should work for core fans, casual viewers and new people. If it only works for experts, or only for beginners, it will not connect with most people

**Mistakes to avoid**

- going broad on day one
- switching niches because you're bored instead of because of results

**You are done when:** You have your niche drawn as a middle with rings, and an honest answer to "could I do this for 10 years?"

**Read more:** [1.5 Niche: start narrow, then widen](guide/01-personal-brand-and-positioning.md#m1-l5) · [1.6 Size your niche: core, casual and new viewers](guide/01-personal-brand-and-positioning.md#m1-l6) · [1.13 Obsession, stickiness and fans](guide/01-personal-brand-and-positioning.md#m1-l13) · [8.14 Starting from zero: niche, bio, voice and category](guide/08-distribution-and-growth.md#m8-l14)

### Position yourself

> **Goal:** Make it clear in one sentence what you do, who it's for, and why people should pick you

**Answer these**

1. fill in: I help [who] with [what] in [what way]
2. what do you stand for, who is it for, why should they care, and why can't they get it anywhere else?
3. which feeling should people have when they see your posts?
4. what are you really great at?
5. who else could say what you say? If many people can, what do you have that they don't: experience, access, results, a different view or good taste?
6. can you list 10 pieces of original work around your position (things to build, test, explain, measure or argue about)?
7. which 5 things do you want people to link with you?
8. which 3 things do you refuse to be linked with?
9. what do you promise, what proves it, what's your view on the topic, and what's your personality?

**Rules from the sources**

- a brand is a link in people's heads, built by repeating the same pair of things again and again, like your name and "hooks"
- pair yourself with your 5 links in every piece you make
- look for the gap just under a popular topic: what do people keep asking, and where does existing content stop before it's useful?
- positioning has 4 parts: what you believe, clear messaging, signal (how much of your content is truly valuable) and taste (consistent choices over time)

**Mistakes to avoid**

- taking a position many others could claim too
- changing your message every month, so no link sticks

**You are done when:** You have one sentence about what you do, 5 things people link with you, 3 things you refuse to be linked with, and a list of 10 pieces of original work

**Read more:** [1.4 What a brand really is: a link in people's minds](guide/01-personal-brand-and-positioning.md#m1-l4) · [1.7 Positioning: promise, proof, perspective, personality](guide/01-personal-brand-and-positioning.md#m1-l7) · [1.9 Finding your gap and your advantage](guide/01-personal-brand-and-positioning.md#m1-l9)

<a id="s6"></a>

### Build your point of view

> **Goal:** Give people something to agree with, so they remember you

**Answer these**

1. what are 5 things people in your niche say that you disagree with, and what do you believe instead?
2. which of those could you keep saying even if nobody liked it?
3. which belief or habit does your content stand against? (an enemy, but never a person)
4. what do you want people to believe before they see your offer?
5. what do you teach that deserves a name? (2 to 4 words, easy to remember, repeat and point to)
6. which vague word in your niche can you explain in a way that tells people what to do?

**Rules from the sources**

- keep 3 to 5 beliefs that go against the usual advice, and repeat them. Write them in a list so you can reuse them
- it's fine to disagree with what most people think. The enemy is a belief or a habit, and it is never a person
- test your opinions in your content and keep pushing the one people react to most
- a topic gives you things to post about. A point of view gives those posts a direction
- build a world around your brand: named ideas, your own language, and characters and places people recognise

**Mistakes to avoid**

- strong opinions you don't actually believe
- opinions everyone in your niche already says

**You are done when:** You have a list of 3 to 5 beliefs and at least 1 named idea

**Read more:** [1.8 Contrarian takes and a point of view](guide/01-personal-brand-and-positioning.md#m1-l8) · [1.11 Worldbuilding: a world your audience lives in](guide/01-personal-brand-and-positioning.md#m1-l11) · [4.7 Making old ideas feel new](guide/04-psychology-and-attention.md#m4-l7) · [4.11 Identity, sharing, common enemies and disagreement](guide/04-psychology-and-attention.md#m4-l11)

### Show the person behind it

> **Goal:** Let people get to know you, because people follow people and not ideas

**Answer these**

1. where were you, what made you change, and where are you now? (3 lines)
2. what are 3 beliefs about your topic that you hold strongly?
3. which 5 surprising true facts about your life could start a story?
4. what loss or miss can you share, how did it feel, and what can someone learn from it?
5. what will you share about your life, and what stays private?
6. what's your purpose, your pain, your real interests and the things you will never give up?
7. what do you want the next chapters of your story to be?

**Rules from the sources**

- your story is what makes the same idea feel different when you say it
- either tell a story as the topic itself, or start a topic that already works with your own story
- a story about a hard time builds trust only when the audience gets a takeaway from it
- share real weaknesses only, and never fake ones
- write one line every night about the story of your day, and keep a story bank: title, point, short summary, where to use it and a rating

**Mistakes to avoid**

- only posting wins, which starts to feel fake
- sharing personal details with no takeaway

**You are done when:** You have a 3-line brand story, 5 surprising facts, 1 loss with a takeaway and a list of what stays private

**Read more:** [1.10 Being real, your story and who you are](guide/01-personal-brand-and-positioning.md#m1-l10) · [3.12 Finding, writing and editing your stories](guide/03-storytelling.md#m3-l12) · [3.13 Your brand as a story](guide/03-storytelling.md#m3-l13) · [3.14 Trust through transformation, losses and takeaways](guide/03-storytelling.md#m3-l14)

### Plan your content

> **Goal:** Turn your brand into a map of what to post, so you never start from zero

**Answer these**

1. which 2 to 3 broad topics will you cover, and which 3 to 5 smaller topics sit under each? (your content map)
2. which 3 to 4 pillars (main themes) support your one sentence about what you do?
3. what is each pillar's job: get you discovered, build trust or sell?
4. which 1 to 3 formats will you use (for example short video, text post, article)? Are they made for your platform, and do you enjoy making them?
5. write each one as a formula: topic + format + look of the post
6. how will you split your content between posts that already worked, new versions of them, and experiments?

**Rules from the sources**

- pick 3 to 4 topics and 1 to 3 formats, and combine them into 5 to 10 formulas you can repeat
- change the idea, keep the structure. Keep the layout and swap the idea
- give every post one job: reach new people, build trust or make a sale. A rough split is 60% reach, 30% trust and 10% sales, changed to fit your business
- aim for about 70% content that already worked, 20% new versions of it and 10% experiments
- short posts are where you test ideas fast. The winners become long pieces

**Mistakes to avoid**

- too many formats at once
- pillars with no job

**You are done when:** You have a content map, 3 to 4 pillars with a job each, and 1 to 3 formulas

**Read more:** [6.1 Choosing your formats](guide/06-formats-and-video.md#m6-l1) · [1.9 Finding your gap and your advantage](guide/01-personal-brand-and-positioning.md#m1-l9) · [10.9 The funnel: from stranger to buyer](guide/10-money-and-careers-in-content.md#m10-l9) · [5.17 Doing more of what works, and testing new things](guide/05-ideas-packaging-and-virality.md#m5-l17)

### Set up your platform, pace and profile

> **Goal:** Win on one platform first, at a pace you can keep for a year

**Answer these**

1. where does your person already spend time?
2. are you best at writing, talking or being on camera?
3. how many hours a week do you really have?
4. which one platform will you focus on for the next 6 months, and what is your main format there?
5. what pace could you keep for 6 months without making your posts worse?
6. what does your bio say: who you help, with what, plus 1 line of proof?
7. where will you collect emails, and which free resource will you offer in exchange?

**Rules from the sources**

- master one platform first, then add a second
- the full setup is 1 short-form platform for reaching people, 1 long-form platform for going deeper, and 1 email list or community so you can stay in touch
- first get quality to 7 out of 10. Then raise how much you post to the normal level for the platform. Then push quality to 9
- leave 2 to 3 hours between posts so each one gets its chance
- start an email list on day one. A platform you don't own can change its rules or ban you overnight

**Mistakes to avoid**

- spreading across 4 platforms before winning on one
- a bio that says what you are instead of who you help

**You are done when:** You have one platform, one main format, a weekly pace, a bio and a place to collect emails

**Read more:** [8.5 Choosing your platforms](guide/08-distribution-and-growth.md#m8-l5) · [8.6 How often and when to post](guide/08-distribution-and-growth.md#m8-l6) · [6.12 How often to post: volume, quality and batching](guide/06-formats-and-video.md#m6-l12) · [8.12 Own your audience: connect your channels](guide/08-distribution-and-growth.md#m8-l12) · [8.14 Starting from zero: niche, bio, voice and category](guide/08-distribution-and-growth.md#m8-l14)

## B. Make every piece

*Run every post through these 4 steps*

<a id="s10"></a>

### Pick the idea

> **Goal:** You can't fix a weak topic by editing, so the idea gets most of your time

**Answer these**

1. demand: do enough people care? Fit: does your audience come to you for this? Interest: do you care enough? Score each from 1 to 5 and cut anything with a 1
2. what's the topic, what's your angle on it, and what's the format?
3. is it about your person's life, a small effort for a big reward, different from what everyone says, already proven and doable?
4. will it work for core fans, casual viewers and new people?
5. which pillar does it fit, and what's its job: reach, trust or sale?
6. has it worked before, for you or for someone else? (Compare someone else's post to that account's usual results, and not to big accounts.)
7. what is at risk for the reader: what do they win or lose?

**Rules from the sources**

- the topic is the most important choice. Great packaging can't save a topic nobody wants
- 1 topic x 3 angles x 3 formats = 9 ideas
- copy the format and use your own words, and look at recent posts that did far better than that account usually does
- write 20 or more ideas, throw most away, and make the best few
- everything needs something at risk: give every intro a consequence or an enemy

**Mistakes to avoid**

- making the first idea you had
- copying someone's content instead of their format

**You are done when:** Your idea is written as topic + angle + format, with its job

**Read more:** [5.2 Topic is king](guide/05-ideas-packaging-and-virality.md#m5-l2) · [5.4 What an idea is made of: topic, angle, format](guide/05-ideas-packaging-and-virality.md#m5-l4) · [5.7 Finding outliers](guide/05-ideas-packaging-and-virality.md#m5-l7) · [5.9 Choosing which ideas to make](guide/05-ideas-packaging-and-virality.md#m5-l9) · [5.19 One big video instead of 10 normal ones, and the 3 stages of packaging](guide/05-ideas-packaging-and-virality.md#m5-l19)

<a id="s11"></a>

### Package it and write the hook

> **Goal:** Packaging (the title, image and first line) decides whether anyone sees the idea, so it gets the next most time

**Answer these**

1. can a stranger tell what it's about from the first line alone?
2. why should they care right now?
3. does it start with the benefit or the pain, and not just the name of the thing?
4. could the hook be read two ways? If yes, rewrite it
5. did you write 5 to 20 versions and pick the best?
6. do the title, the image and the first line all say the same thing?
7. for a long piece: is the promise big, specific and timely, and can you deliver it?

**Rules from the sources**

- say what it's about in the first sentence, in as few words as possible
- in 2 to 4 lines, start with the problem, add a "but", then end with a clear promise. Only promise what you can deliver
- the same idea can flop or do great depending on how it's packaged
- write the hook last, and don't keep the first line the AI gives you
- make the spoken words, visuals, text and audio say the same thing

**Mistakes to avoid**

- warm-up lines before the point
- hooks that confuse, feel unrelated to the reader or don't make people care

**You are done when:** Your first line makes sense to a stranger in one second

**Read more:** [2.1 What a hook does and why it decides everything](guide/02-hooks.md#m2-l1) · [2.2 The master formula: lean, stun, snap back](guide/02-hooks.md#m2-l2) · [2.7 Hook mistakes 1 and 2: delay and confusion](guide/02-hooks.md#m2-l7) · [2.14 Hooks in writing: first lines for posts, threads and articles](guide/02-hooks.md#m2-l14) · [5.10 Why packaging decides the result](guide/05-ideas-packaging-and-virality.md#m5-l10)

<a id="s12"></a>

### Write it and keep the promise

> **Goal:** Give what the hook promised, in words anyone can follow

**Answer these**

1. do the first lines after the hook repeat the promise, give one proof and a first small win?
2. is every line simple enough for anyone to understand?
3. can you delete any word without changing the meaning?
4. did you use numbers, names and timeframes instead of "a few" or "soon"?
5. does every tip say what the reader gets from it?
6. for a story: is something at risk, and did every "and then" become "but" or "therefore"?
7. did you give everything the packaging promised?

**Rules from the sources**

- keep your ideas smart and your words simple
- write like you talk. Your voice is the one thing nobody can copy
- raise the next question as you answer the last one, so people keep reading
- show proof before you teach, and be honest about what the piece does not cover
- the piece has to give what the title and hook promised
- add text boxes people can screenshot, so the piece gets saved and shared

**Mistakes to avoid**

- a hook the body doesn't deliver on
- vague words like "stuff" or "things" where a real example should be

**You are done when:** Every line earns its place and the promise is kept

**Read more:** [2.10 After the hook: keep the promise, give the answer at the right time](guide/02-hooks.md#m2-l10) · [11.2 Clear over clever: smart ideas, simple words](guide/11-writing-and-copywriting.md#m11-l2) · [11.4 Every word earns its place](guide/11-writing-and-copywriting.md#m11-l4) · [11.5 Be specific: numbers, names and proof](guide/11-writing-and-copywriting.md#m11-l5) · [3.2 Context plus conflict](guide/03-storytelling.md#m3-l2) · [6.16 Viewer satisfaction: give what the title and thumbnail promised](guide/06-formats-and-video.md#m6-l16)

<a id="s13"></a>

### Check it before you post

> **Goal:** Catch what would hurt trust or stop people sharing before the post goes out

**Answer these**

1. would people share it, and what does sharing it say about them?
2. do you believe everything you're saying?
3. is every claim, number and story true? Did you check the original source of any study?
4. what's this post's one job: reach, trust or sale?
5. would someone save it and come back to it?
6. could anything in it make someone mute, block or report you?

**Rules from the sources**

- people share what says something good about them
- treat stats that spread fast as claims until you find the original source
- if you sell something, aim for trust and not for getting millions of views
- make the size of any ask fit the trust you've built, and don't invent urgency

**Mistakes to avoid**

- repeating a shaky stat because everyone else does
- a post with no clear job

**You are done when:** It's true, it has one job, and you'd be proud to put your name on it

## C. Grow, earn and keep going

*Every week and every month*

<a id="s14"></a>

### Get eyes on it

> **Goal:** Posting alone is not growth. Put your work in front of people who don't know you yet

**Answer these**

1. where will you put this post in front of new people this week?
2. which bigger accounts in your niche can you reply to with a real point of view?
3. whose ideas did you use, and did you credit them?
4. which past winner can you repost, reshape or turn into a long piece?
5. who could you team up with, or share a group chat with?

**Rules from the sources**

- reply and quote to add your own view, and don't write just "great post"
- credit the people whose ideas you use. It builds relationships and gives you some of their reach
- every month, rewrite your winners from new angles: shorter, longer, as a list, with a better hook
- add depth (long pieces) and new platforms only with your best ideas

**Mistakes to avoid**

- posting and walking away
- empty replies that add nothing

**You are done when:** Every post has a plan to reach new people, not just your followers

**Read more:** [8.1 The one rule of growth: get people to see your work](guide/08-distribution-and-growth.md#m8-l1) · [8.7 Replies, comments and networking](guide/08-distribution-and-growth.md#m8-l7) · [8.8 Borrowing reach: quotes, remixes, collabs and group chats](guide/08-distribution-and-growth.md#m8-l8) · [8.9 Reusing content: the waterfall versus the lake](guide/08-distribution-and-growth.md#m8-l9) · [8.10 Do more of what works](guide/08-distribution-and-growth.md#m8-l10)

<a id="s15"></a>

### Measure and learn

> **Goal:** Turn your results into instructions for the next post

**Answer these**

1. for your last 20 posts: how many views, follows, leads (people who show interest in buying) and sales did each bring?
2. what do your top 5 posts share that your bottom 5 don't?
3. for your best post: why did people start reading, where did they leave, and which line got a reaction?
4. which format is worth keeping: does it do well, bring the right people, do you enjoy it and are you proud of it?
5. what will you do more of, and less of, next month?

**Rules from the sources**

- views are an early sign. Follows, leads and money are the real goal
- look at your numbers every day, week, month and 3 months, but change things monthly and not daily
- judge a format only after enough tries, because early results can fool you. One creator says they waited for 100 posts
- treat advice about how the algorithm (the system that decides what people see) works as unproven, and test it on your own account

**Mistakes to avoid**

- judging by views alone
- changing everything after one bad day

**You are done when:** You have 3 instructions for next month, taken from your own best posts

<a id="s16"></a>

### Turn trust into money

> **Goal:** Make it easy for the right people to pay you once they trust you

**Answer these**

1. what do you sell, or what will you sell? (a service, a product or brand deals)
2. fill in: helping [who] go from [where they're stuck] to [where they want to be] so they can [benefit]
3. is the result worth far more than the price?
4. what's the next step in your content: a free resource, a sales page or a call?
5. what's the smallest version you could build and sell in a week for about \$10?
6. of your last 10 posts, how many gave something and how many asked for something?
7. how can someone say they're interested: a questionnaire, a DM or a reply?

**Rules from the sources**

- give first, sell later, and keep the ask much smaller than what you gave
- give away your best ideas and sell the help with using them
- views turn into money only when 4 things are true: people feel "that's my problem", they trust you, there's a clear next step, and the offer fits. If one is missing, you earn nothing
- with a small audience, sell by hand: posts, a newsletter, a questionnaire, DMs and a link to pay
- test an offer before you build it: post about the problem, run a poll, show how you work, start a waitlist, or sell it softly before it exists

**Mistakes to avoid**

- selling before people trust you
- inventing urgency

**You are done when:** You have one clear promise, one next step in your content and a way for people to say they're interested

### Keep going for the long run

> **Goal:** Most brands fail because the person stops, so plan for the long run from day one

**Answer these**

1. what's your plan for the next 60 days: topics, formats, posts per week and a review date?
2. what will you do when a whole month of posts does badly?
3. what would make you quit, and how will you stop that from happening?
4. where do you keep your ideas, hooks, questions and stories so you never start from zero?
5. which day of the week is your content day, and how far ahead do you make posts in one go?
6. which steps could AI speed up, and where do you keep the final say?

**Rules from the sources**

- expect it to take about 36 months to get real results. One creator says this, so treat it as a rough guide
- test widely in the first 60 days (about 50 posts). Then keep only the formats that do well, bring the right people, you enjoy and you're proud of
- a rough timeline: 30 days until "I can do this", 100 days until it's a habit, 365 days until you can expect the first money
- fix your process before you blame your creativity, and make it simple before you automate it
- let AI handle the speed and keep the taste for yourself, with a human approval step

**Mistakes to avoid**

- quitting before the repeated effort adds up
- automating a process that doesn't work yet

**You are done when:** You have a 60-day plan with a review date, one place for ideas and a weekly content day
