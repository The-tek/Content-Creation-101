# Module 8: Distribution and growth

Get people to see your work, then turn their attention into trust, an audience you own, and sales

Posting alone doesn't grow an account. Growth comes from getting your content in front of people, through replies, quote posts and collabs, and then keeping them on channels you own, like an email list. Pick one platform, do more of what works, and measure sales, not just views

## Lesson 8.1: The one rule of growth: get people to see your work

### Big idea

Posting alone does nothing. Growth comes from putting your work in front of people, and from making it good enough that they pass it on for you

### How it works

#### Why posting is not enough

Many beginners post and hope the app will find them. Most of the time it won't. You have to put your content in front of people, ideally people who already have an audience and will share it

If nobody sees your work, nothing else counts. Building things has become easy, but getting noticed hasn't, and a good product nobody hears about is a product nobody buys

It's fine to post to nobody at the start, as practice. But soon you need to put real effort into getting people to see what you make

#### Three parts of success

First, what you make and whether it is useful. Second, how many people know it exists. Third, whether it is useful to the people who do know

If the third part is weak, you fix it with marketing and sales, which just means explaining the value better and asking people to act

So post often, keep improving your work, and keep growing the number of people who know about it

#### Distribution is trust at a large scale

Distribution means getting your work in front of lots of people who trust it. Another way to say it: other people tell your story for you

If what you make is really good, people share it because it made them think. If it's generic, posting more often won't save it

Let AI do the boring jobs (formatting, scheduling, checking numbers), and spend your own energy on making something worth sharing

#### The four steps of growth

1. Make content that makes people feel something or learn something, so they want to act
2. Get people to see it, with replies, quote posts, collabs and group chats (later lessons cover each)
3. Every month, look at your results and come up with better ideas from what did well
4. Take your best ideas and make them deeper (articles, long videos) and wider (more platforms), so you don't depend on one platform

#### When views are low, look at your content first

An algorithm mostly follows what people are interested in. So instead of "the algorithm isn't picking up my video", try "people aren't connecting with my message"

Getting 100 to 300 views is usually not a shadowban (a secret penalty from the app). Most often it means people didn't want that piece

### Mistakes

- posting and waiting for the algorithm to find you
- blaming a shadowban when people simply didn't want that piece
- trying to fix weak, generic work by posting more

### Try it

Answer three questions in one line each for your own content: what is useful about it, roughly how many people know it exists, and who it is useful to. Then list 3 ways to put your next post in front of new people this week

## Lesson 8.2: Interest media: how apps pick who sees you

### Big idea

Feeds now show people what they are interested in, not only what the people they follow post. The app tests each post on strangers, so any post can win, and the wrong kind of views can hurt you

### How it works

#### From follower feeds to interest feeds

About five years ago you mostly saw posts from people you followed. Now your feed shows what the app thinks you are interested in, mostly from people you don't follow. This is called interest media, and it started with TikTok

A small account and a huge account now compete for the same attention. Small creators can grow faster than ever, and big ones face more competition

So judge each post on its own views, not on your follower count. Even your third post can change your whole career

#### How the app tests your post

This is a simple model from one creator, not official rules, so the numbers are rough

1. The app looks at your visuals, audio, caption and account, and guesses who would like the post
2. It shows the post to a small first group (about 200 people), mostly people who don't follow you. If strangers like it, that is the real signal
3. If the numbers are good, it shows the post to about 2,000 people, then 20,000, and so on. If they are bad, it stops. "200 view jail" means the first group didn't like it

The app reads how long people watch, how many watch to the end, and how many people like, comment and share compared to the views

#### Stay on the same few topics

One idea per post and one main topic per account help, because mixed topics make it hard for the app to know who to show a post to

Say one account mixes tech and marketing. Each post only interests half of the audience, so it may get views but not grow

#### The wrong views can hurt you

A viral post on a broad topic mostly reaches people who scroll fast and move on. A specific post reaches people who actually care or buy

Imagine a page about Claude posts a reel for marketers (50,000 views, 500 new followers), then a funny gorilla video (5 million views). Its next product video gets shown to the gorilla crowd. They don't react, and the app stops pushing it

Views only help if they turn into followers who want your main topic

### Where creators disagree

Some creators say posting time, hashtags and captions don't change what the algorithm does. Others tested it and found posting time did change results (see the lesson on how often and when to post)

### Mistakes

- judging a post by your follower count instead of its own views
- mixing unrelated topics on one account
- chasing broad viral hits that bring followers who don't care about your main topic

### Try it

Take your last 10 posts and mark each one "main topic" or "off topic". Next to each, write whether it brought followers. Look for any off-topic post with lots of views and few follows, and write one line on what kind of audience it may have attracted

## Lesson 8.3: How the X algorithm works, as creators read it

### Big idea

X has shared parts of its code, and creators read it as three stages: scouts find posts, a judge scores them by what each viewer will probably do, and a filter removes the rest. Shares, replies and quotes count far more than likes. This is creators' reading, not official rules

### How it works

#### How sure can we be?

Everything here comes from creators who read X's open code and say what they think it means. They don't work at X, and X keeps some parts private

The weights (how much each action counts) change often and without warning. Treat every number as "it appears that", not as fact

#### Stage 1: scouts find posts

X builds the For You feed from a few sources of posts

- people you already follow
- people you don't follow yet, based on a guess of what you will like. This is where fast growth comes from
- posts popular in your niche. X groups people by who reacts to what, so a post spreads inside its interest group first
- a shared list of popular recent posts

#### Stage 2: the judge scores each post

For every post and every viewer, a model predicts how likely that person is to like, reply, share, follow and so on. Then it adds up a weighted score

These are weights as one creator read them in late 2026: copying the link to share 20, reply 5, quote 5, share by DM 5, follow 4, like 0.66. Negative actions cost far more: blocking the author -31, tapping "not interested" -52, reporting -234

So shares are the strongest signal, replies and quotes count about 7 times as much as a like, and one report cancels about 350 likes

#### Stage 3: the filter removes posts

Before you see them, X removes posts older than 48 hours, posts you already saw, blocked and muted accounts, spam and bots

X also scores how trusted your account is, based on who follows you and reacts to you. The quality of your followers counts, not just how many you have

To stay safe, don't let AI write and post all of your text, and avoid content that gets you muted, blocked or reported

#### What to do with this

Ask before you post: would someone send this to a friend? Copying the link to send is one of the strongest signals

Write posts that start conversations, because replies and quotes are strong. Give some posts a clear reason to follow you

Start video with your strongest moment and cut long intros, because people who open it and leave right away don't help

X is getting better at telling real interest from empty reactions. It wants better content, not more content

### Where creators disagree

Some creators say the first 2 hours of a post decide everything. Others describe a 48-hour window and say to reply on your own post in the first 30 minutes. On bookmarks, one creator says they have little effect on ranking, while another still asks 'would someone bookmark this?' as a sign a post is worth spreading. Both can be true: a save can show value without pushing the ranking

### Mistakes

- chasing likes, one of the weakest signals
- posting things that get mutes, blocks, reports or "not interested" taps
- treating any creator's weight table as official or permanent

### Try it

Pick one post you're about to publish. Decide which action you want most (link share, DM share, reply, quote or follow) and change one line to make it more likely. Then check the post for anything that might get a "not interested" tap

## Lesson 8.4: Growing on X: what to do and what to avoid

### Big idea

X rewards posts people share, reply to and quote. Make original posts on one idea, and avoid anything that gets you muted, reported or flagged as fake

### How it works

#### Why creators take X seriously

X is the best platform for meeting people and testing ideas quickly. It suits writers, founders and business owners, and most creators still treat it as a side platform

Posts that start a small fight in the replies, like contrarian takes, one-line opinions and predictions, tend to win. Feel-good posts that try to be liked instead of right tend to lose

Posts die within about a day, and an account can turn around in a few days, so you can test and improve fast

#### What to do

Make content people want to share: easy to scan and easy to send. Before you post, ask: would someone reply to it, or send it to a friend?

Give real value from your own experience, your contacts and your job. Nobody else has these, so nobody can copy them

Keep one idea per post, with a clear takeaway, a specific person it is for and a CTA (the one thing you want the reader to do next)

Write hooks that are strong but honest. If the first line promises something the post doesn't deliver, people leave and the algorithm punishes it

Test the formats X pushes, like threads and articles, and build a close group of good people around you, because the algorithm reads who reacts to you

#### Five ways to get comments

- take a hard stance
- take the opposite side of what most people say
- push the wording further. "Better than every pasta shop in the world" gets more reactions than "good pasta"
- talk about things people already have opinions on. Nike gets more reactions than "shoes"
- make people feel something

#### What to avoid

- engagement bait (begging for likes and replies), like "like if you agree" or "comment YES to receive it"
- hashtag stuffing, because hashtags are not a growth plan anymore
- repeating the same text again and again, which looks like spam. Use the idea again with a new angle instead
- negativity, politics and arguing with trolls (people who only want a fight). Reports and mutes lower how X rates your account a lot, so block or mute them
- bots, automated likes or follows, low-effort AI replies, and re-uploading other people's content

#### Links, CTAs and Creator Rewards

X punishes posts that send people off the platform. Keep only about 10% of your posts about selling, and put the CTA in the replies

Creator Rewards pays you for impressions (times your post was shown) from people outside your network. AI-written posts, engagement groups and re-uploaded content block it

Basics for your profile: get verified, show your face, write a 2 to 3 line bio of what you do and who it is for, and pin a post that gets people to follow or buy

### Where creators disagree

Some creators say you should get verified and show your face, and that being faceless is the hard way. Even they admit a faceless brand can work if you're good at content, but that most people aren't willing to put their name out there. So showing your face is easier, not required

### Mistakes

- engagement bait like "comment YES"
- posting the same text again and again
- arguing with trolls instead of muting or blocking them
- putting the sales link in the main post instead of the replies

### Try it

Go through your last 10 posts and mark any that break a rule above (engagement bait, two ideas in one post, a link in the main post, repeated text, negativity). Rewrite one so it has one idea, one reader and a reason to share

## Lesson 8.5: Choosing your platforms

### Big idea

Win on one platform before you spread out. Pick it by where your audience already is, what you are good at, how much time you have, and the format that platform pushes hardest

### How it works

#### Rented, owned and earned channels

Rented channels are platforms you don't control, like X, TikTok, Instagram and YouTube. They are best for reach, which means finding new people

Owned channels are audiences you reach directly, like a newsletter, a community or a website. They are best for trust and sales

Earned channels are other people's audiences, like podcasts where you are a guest, or collabs. They are best for getting your name known

Use rented platforms to find people and owned channels to keep them

#### How to pick your platform

Start with 1 to 2 platforms at most. Ask three questions:

1. Where does my audience already spend time?
2. Am I better at writing, talking or being on camera?
3. How much time can I really give?

The best platform is often the one you already use. You know how it feels, and growing an account takes so long that you'll hate it if you don't enjoy the app

#### How many channels for your time

- little time: 1 to 2 channels
- about 8 to 12 hours a week: 2 to 4 channels
- a lot of time: 5 or more, but only if you repurpose (turn one piece of content into many)

Pick one platform to focus on for the next 6 to 12 months. Focus multiplies your results, and spread across 7 platforms you get weak results on all of them

#### The format each platform pushes

Each platform has a hero format, the format it pushes hardest

- Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and Snapchat: short video
- YouTube: videos of 10 to 30 minutes
- X and Threads: short text
- LinkedIn: short text, and now short video too

For writers, social media is the easiest way to get readers. It's free, you can post every day, and you don't need video or design skills. X is a good pick if you sell to developers, startups or tech buyers

#### Don't spread out too early

Stay on one platform and one format until your system has run for 30 days in a row without breaking. Spread over 5 platforms, each one gets a fifth of your effort and your message changes on each

Once one platform works, a simple plan is: 1 short-form platform for reach (about 7 posts a week), 1 long-form platform for depth (1 to 2 pieces a week), and 1 channel to keep in touch with people (email or a community)

### Where creators disagree

Where to start: some creators say start with short form, where it's easier and you control more, then move people to long form. Others say start on a long-form platform like X or YouTube, then turn ideas into short form after 2 to 3 months. Best platform: one creator picks Substack for writers, another picks Instagram and YouTube, another picks X for tech buyers

### Mistakes

- starting on 5 or 7 platforms at once
- picking a platform you never use yourself
- picking a format the platform doesn't push

### Try it

Answer the three questions in writing: where your audience spends time, whether you're best at writing, talking or camera, and how many hours a week you really have. Then name the one platform you'll focus on for the next 6 months and its hero format

## Lesson 8.6: How often and when to post

### Big idea

Creators disagree on how much to post and when. They agree on this: pick a pace you can keep, leave space between posts so each one gets its chance, and put quality first

### How it works

#### Two routes on X

The quantity route is up to 24 posts a day, at least 1 hour apart. It is only for news accounts or very fast niches like AI

Many posts will flop, and you hope 1 or 2 a day take off

The quality route is a few posts a day, at least 2 hours apart, with each post getting its own reach before the next one goes out

Don't post several things back to back, because they compete for the same audience

#### Quality first, then volume

One great post beats six average ones, so spend your time making one idea great

A simple order: first get quality to about 7 out of 10. Then post as often as is normal for the platform (short video 1 a day, YouTube 1 a week, short text about 5 a day)

Then push quality to 9 out of 10 and keep it steady for 6 months

Huge volume does work for big brands and teams, but early on daily posts mainly help you find your winning topics: make content, look at the results, make more of the winners

#### When to post

One test on YouTube Shorts found that the day of the week made no difference, but morning posts got clearly more views

Don't copy someone else's best time. In YouTube Studio, open Analytics, then Audience, to see when your own audience is most active, and post then

#### Steady beats perfect

A fixed schedule doesn't change much for the algorithm, but it builds the habit of posting and helps you stay with it

Posting steadily also builds the link between you and your topic, the way people link a sports star to one shoe brand because he wore it in every game

If daily is too much, post less. Just don't go so long without posting that you lose momentum

### Examples

A YouTube Shorts frequency test:

One creator tested two channels from twice a week up to four times a day. At four a day only about half of the videos got picked up and quality fell

Once a day gave time for quality and steady reach

Why it works: More posts doesn't automatically mean more reach

### Where creators disagree

Advice on volume ranges from 5 posts a day to 20 or more, while others say one great post beats six average ones. Choose a pace you can keep for years

### Mistakes

- posting several posts back to back so they compete
- copying another creator's best posting time instead of checking your own audience
- posting more before the quality is decent

### Try it

Write down the pace you could keep for 6 months without dropping quality. Then plan tomorrow's posts with at least 2 hours between them, and after a week compare the posts that had space with any that went out close together

## Lesson 8.7: Replies, comments and networking

### Big idea

Replies and real relationships are one of the cheapest ways to get seen. They only work when your reply is interesting, comes from your own experience, and goes under posts people actually read

### How it works

#### Replies that get profile clicks

If your reply grabs attention and gives value, readers click your profile and follow you. Keep replying for months, because people need to see your name again and again

Reply to posts that start discussions. A post can get lots of likes while only 100 people open the replies, because nothing in it starts a conversation

Don't repeat what the original post said. Talk about yourself and how you lived the same thing. That gets the most profile clicks

#### Who to reply to

Small accounts get fewer impressions (times a post is shown), so replying only to accounts your size won't grow you much

Also reply to bigger accounts, with something interesting. Ask real questions, not "nice post"

#### Building your network

Build a close group of good people in your niche. It helps your reach and how X rates your account

A simple routine:

1. Follow 50 people in your space
2. Reply to 10 big ones
3. DM 5 to 10 people a day, and give first, without selling

Be social. Ask questions, support people and build real relationships, until they're happy to share your work when you ask. Sounding like a corporate robot in a DM ruins it

#### Talk to your own followers

Making people feel noticed is the cheapest way to earn loyalty. Reply to comments and DMs, say thank you and answer questions

Block 30 to 40 minutes in your calendar for it. Creators who post and then disappear build no relationship and no influence

#### Read your replies

Look through your comments for questions nobody has answered. A comment like "but how do I do this with no money?" is your next piece of content

On X, a reply rate of 1 to 3% (replies divided by views) is excellent

### Mistakes

- replies that repeat the original post
- replying under boring posts nobody opens the replies on
- only replying to accounts your own size
- low-effort AI or spam replies
- posting and then ignoring your own comments

### Try it

Find 3 posts from bigger accounts in your niche that already have a lively reply section. Write one reply to each that shares your own experience of the topic in 2 to 3 lines, without repeating the post. Then spend 10 minutes answering every comment on your own latest post

## Lesson 8.8: Borrowing reach: quotes, remixes, collabs and group chats

### Big idea

The fastest way to reach new people is through people who already have them. Quote and credit bigger creators, make things together with creators whose audience is like yours, and keep your own content good

### How it works

#### Quote posts

A quote post is a repost with your own comment on top. The creator may see it and repost it

A bland "this is great" gives nobody a reason to repost or click. Add something of your own, like a lesson from your experience or a point of disagreement

Quoting a 500,000-follower account is mostly luck, because they probably won't see it. Pick posts where your comment is worth their time

#### Remixing what's already big

Remixing means using what is already popular in your own way, like a DJ remixing a hit song. You borrow the listeners of the original

Quote a creator's idea in your post or newsletter, explain how it changed what you do, credit them, and then send them a DM that you mentioned them. They may share it without being asked

When you help spread other people's names, they help spread yours

#### Collabs

A collab means making something together with another creator, like a podcast episode or a joint post

Only collaborate when the audiences are similar. The wrong niche brings the wrong audience

To find partners, look for channels that make content like yours and compare titles and topics. A good starting plan is one collab a month

#### Group chats and masterminds

Many creators are in group chats with other creators, where they talk strategy and comment on each other's posts. A mastermind (a group that meets to help each other grow) can become a real friendship

If a group feels like only a trade of favours, leave and start a new one

#### Other people as amplifiers

Clips of you shared by others, like podcast clips and reactions, reach more people than you can alone. Make your content easy to clip and share

Named ideas that you coin spread too, because other people use them in their own posts

### Mistakes

- bland quote posts like "this is great"
- collabs with people whose audience is not like yours
- buying follower ads, which can cost you hundreds of followers a day
- group chats that are only a trade of favours

### Try it

Pick one idea from a bigger creator in your niche that changed how you work. Write a post explaining the idea, how it changed what you do and who it came from, with credit

Publish it, then send that creator a short, honest DM saying you mentioned them

## Lesson 8.9: Reusing content: the waterfall versus the lake

### Big idea

One idea can feed many platforms, but creators disagree on how. The waterfall cuts one big piece into many. The lake makes content for each main platform first, then moves only the proven winners

### How it works

#### The content waterfall

The waterfall starts with one main piece, for example a 13-minute YouTube video, and cuts it into 26 or more smaller pieces

Say you film one video. You cut the best moments into short videos for Reels, TikTok and Shorts, write posts for LinkedIn and X from the script, and send a newsletter later

The payoff is that one video fills the next month's calendar, so you aren't always making something new from scratch

#### Adapt it for each platform

Don't post identical text everywhere. It looks like spam on X

Use a different hook and caption on each app, and write the way that platform's own users write

#### The lake method

The problem with the waterfall is that it improves the content for one platform and gives weak results on the others. Say a YouTube video with 100 views gets clipped into Instagram clips

It spreads a weak piece to more places

With the lake, you pick the main platforms that serve your goals and make content for each one. Everything goes into the lake

Then you fish out the best performers and post them on your other platforms. An Instagram winner goes to LinkedIn, for example

The simple rule: you'd rather not post than post something you already know will fail

#### Which one to use

- Some creators say the waterfall works less than it did a year ago, so each platform needs its own plan
- A fair mix is to make content for each main platform first, then move the proven winners to the others

### Mistakes

- posting the exact same text on every platform
- cutting up a main piece that never did well on its home platform
- posting something everywhere when you already know it will flop

### Try it

Take your best piece from the last 3 months. List where it could go next, and for each place write the new hook or caption it would need

Then drop any place where you'd skip posting if you knew the piece would flop

## Lesson 8.10: Do more of what works

### Big idea

Your best growth ideas are usually your own past hits. Spot them with the right numbers, then repost, re-edit, re-record and reuse them instead of hunting for brand new ideas

### How it works

#### Spot your winners

Review your posts in batches of 10 and sort each batch by follows gained. Then copy what the winner did differently

Compare your top 20% of posts with your bottom 20% (topics, hooks, words). One creator found this way that the audience wanted systems over mindset, and that specific numbers in hooks did about 10 times better

Two numbers help you read a post. Likes divided by impressions: 2 to 3% is a big hit. Follows divided by profile visits: most profiles get about 5%

After each post, write 3 notes: why people started reading, where they left, and which line got a reaction. If you can't say why it worked, you can't repeat it

#### Make more of what worked

Every month, rewrite your best posts from new angles: shorter, longer, as a list, with a better hook

When a format or topic takes off, push it hard. It might only be hot for 30, 60 or 90 days, so go all in while it works

People link you to the things they see you repeat, so repeating your hits builds that link. Most of your audience hasn't seen your old posts or doesn't remember them

#### The 4 Rs

- repost: put every winner up again after 60 days, then again 60 days after that
- re-edit: change a winner slightly. Keep the hook and change the middle
- re-record: same winning topic, new angle, new shoot. Take your top 5 from the last 6 months and film them again
- repurpose: move your winners to other platforms

On X, don't repost identical text, because it looks like spam. Re-edit or re-record gives the same gain safely

#### Find a format, then repeat it fast

On channels grown from scratch, most of the growth often comes from 1 or 2 formats. Try an idea, and if it works, do it again as fast as you can

Twice your average views is enough of a signal. If it works twice in a row, you have a winning format

The worry about boring the audience is mostly a myth. People overestimate how much viewers want something new, and underestimate how much they want more of what they already like

#### Know when to stop

A signature series (a type of post you are known for) works at most about 3 times a month, or it dies

Formulas wear out, so keep testing even when the old things work

### Mistakes

- getting a hit and never repeating its topic, format or angle
- assuming your audience remembers everything you posted
- running a winning series so often that it dies
- reading one number and guessing why something worked

### Try it

Sort your last 30 posts by follows gained (or by views if you can't see follows). Take the top one and write 3 new versions: a different hook, a different format (list, story or thread), and a shorter or deeper version. Schedule one for next week

## Lesson 8.11: Building an audience that spreads you

### Big idea

Followers are not the goal. An active audience comes back, replies and shares without being asked. You build it by being reliably useful, giving generously and making people feel part of something

### How it works

#### Passive audience or active community

A follower count looks good but proves little. An active community is made of people who reply, share without being asked and come back daily

You earn it by being useful, saying specific things, being honest about what you know and don't know, and sticking around long enough. AI can shrink making content from days to minutes, but trust still takes months or years

Casual fans watch and leave. Super fans want to help you grow and share you

#### Steady value brings people back

People come back to a source that is useful again and again, with value they can't easily find elsewhere. Viewers who act on your advice and get results come back over and over

Think of a watering hole. An elephant goes back to the hole that had water 9 times out of 10, not the one that had water 1 time in 3

Every useful post is water

Evergreen content, which someone can come back to years later, lasts longer than news. Make it easy to binge with a series, so people have more to watch after the first piece

#### Give generously

Give more than feels normal. Share the knowledge, and sell the personal help

Think of boxing: jab, jab, jab, then the strong right hook. Give value several times before you ask for anything

If every post is a sales pitch, people learn to avoid you

#### Make people feel part of it

Make people feel special with a name, public thanks and shared language. Thank people, reshare them and meet them, and they help you grow

Give them a home: Discord for real-time chat, Skool for courses plus community, or a private group. A newsletter is the best way in, but it isn't a community on its own

A common enemy helps the right people join. It should be an idea, never a specific person or company

### Mistakes

- chasing follower count instead of people who reply and share
- selling in every post
- being useful only some of the time, like a watering hole that is often dry
- treating a newsletter alone as a community

### Try it

Look at your last 10 posts and mark each one "water" (someone could use it today) or "dry" (filler, promo or vague). Count the water

Then write down one free thing you're holding back that you could give away next week

## Lesson 8.12: Own your audience: connect your channels

### Big idea

Platforms you don't own can change the rules or ban you overnight. Move people onto something you own, like an email list, from day one, and point every channel at the others with a clear reason

### How it works

#### Why you need to own your audience

If a platform banned you tomorrow, your whole audience would be gone. So build a channel you own too, like a newsletter or a community

One creator says X shows a post to about 15% of your followers, while email reaches 100%. Start your list on day one, even if you can't publish regularly

#### Content is a web, not a funnel

People jump between YouTube, Instagram, X and LinkedIn in any order, and may never see your newsletter. That's fine

So point every channel at another one with a reason, like X to your newsletter or short video to long video. Then move people to channels you own

#### From social to email

The path is: social, then email list, then keeping in touch, then selling. Selling straight from a post rarely works

Pin a post with one sign-up offer for one type of reader. Give a free checklist or template first, and the paid product after

Say you post on Instagram that anyone who messages you a keyword gets a free template. People go to your DMs, often follow you too, and you can send them to your list

#### Point to one next step

On YouTube, recommend one next video. Find the next problem your viewer will have, and if this video took them from 0 to 1, point to your 1 to 10 video

Point to only one, because several options confuse people

### Mistakes

- building your whole audience on one platform you don't own
- waiting until you're big to start an email list
- trying to sell straight from a post
- offering several next steps instead of one

### Try it

Pick your best post from the last month. Write one free, matching resource it could lead to (a checklist, template or guide), and draft the reply or pinned post that offers it

Then write down where that sign-up would collect emails

## Lesson 8.13: Patience, practice and not quitting

### Big idea

Most growth comes late. The first months look flat, but the work still counts, and the people who keep posting through the flat stretch are the ones who take off

### How it works

#### Three growth phases

A channel moves through three phases: setting up, improving and fine-tuning

In setting up, you work out who you are as a channel, pick a niche and post one or two videos a week until you have about 30 to 50 videos. Some will do okay, and most will do nothing

In improving, you post at a steady pace and start using data, like retention curves (graphs that show where viewers stop watching). In fine-tuning, you use data to improve what already works

Don't tell a beginner to learn everything at once. Sometimes you just need to keep your head down and make 30, 40, 50 videos

#### Two paths

One coach watched hundreds of creators and describes two paths. Both start the same, with about 1,000 views in month 1

On path 1, people feel it's a waste of time, wait to find a perfect hook, and take a break. The breaks get longer, until they stop

On path 2, people keep posting through the flat stretch and learn the basics. Around month 2 to 3 things start to work, and views start to build

Learning about hooks all the time without posting is like getting ready all day and never going out

#### Practice before numbers

Commit to a number of pieces you're proud of, like 100 newsletters or 100 videos, before you judge the results

Look at your work from six months ago. You should almost cringe, and that is how you know you're improving

Expect a personal brand to take a long time, maybe 36 months and hundreds of posts. Pick a sub-niche and stay in it for at least a year, because changing topic every week stops your growth from building on itself

#### Don't quit right before it works

Growth builds on earlier growth only if you keep going. If you quit, you restart from zero

It takes more time to build the base of a house than to build the rooms on top of it. One channel took about 7 years to reach 100,000 subscribers, then grew past 2 million in the next 2 years

Drops later on feel like disasters, but from far away they look like small bumps. The hardest time is not giving up when things were working and now seem to slip

### Mistakes

- taking breaks to "learn more" until the breaks are longer than the posting
- judging your work in the first 30 days, when you are still learning
- changing topics every week
- buying followers
- quitting just before things start to grow

### Try it

Pick a number of pieces to make before you judge anything (100 is a good start). Count how many you have made so far, and write down how many more you need

Hold your review until you reach it

## Lesson 8.14: Starting from zero: niche, bio, voice and category

### Big idea

At the start, narrow works better than broad. Pick a sub-niche, say who you help in your bio, get known for one thing and repeat it

### How it works

#### Pick a niche you can stay in

Pick a sub-niche, not a huge category like personal development or health, and stay in it for at least a year. A sub-niche is a narrow slice, like "personal development for new managers"

- Pick a topic you could make 100 or more posts on
- If you're a beginner, make 20 to 30 posts on anything you like first, then decide

#### Write a bio for the reader

Your bio is the first thing people read, so write it about the reader, not about you

Say "I write about X for Y", for example "I write about sleep for new parents" (made up). Or say "on a mission to help Y get Z"

Then add 1 line that proves you can help, like a result or a job you've done. Be clear, not clever

#### Get known for one thing

Say the same thing over and over, until it feels boring to you. Own a word, a format you repeat, even a look

You don't need a studio to look believable. One creator says a raw kitchen clip got about 100 times the views of a polished video

To look like you belong, talk about what you've actually done and show yourself doing the work

#### Study what works

Build a swipe file, a folder of the top 100 posts in your niche. Save the hook and the first 30 seconds of each

Copy the hook and the shape, never the content, and put your own knowledge in

Keep a list of your audience's problems in their exact words, like "I don't have the money and I'm scared I'll look stupid asking"

### Mistakes

- picking a huge category as your niche
- a clever bio that hides who it is for
- copying a viral post's content instead of its format
- changing your topic every week

### Try it

Write your bio in the "I write about X for Y" form, then add 1 line that proves you can help. Then write 5 topics you could happily talk about for 30 minutes

## Lesson 8.15: Why the most-viewed article in X history worked

### Big idea

The most-viewed article in X history worked because of a few plain choices: a bold promise with a time limit, a complete system, and points that had already worked as posts

### How it works

#### The promise

The article was called "How to fix your entire life in 1 day". It was 5,000 to 6,000 words, and by its author's own count it got 339,000 likes on X

The promise was extreme and had a time limit. It goes against the common belief that change takes years, which makes people curious

#### A greatest-hits album

Each of its 7 sections was an idea that had already done well as a post. Every idea inside the article is a reason it could spread, not just the title

#### The shape

1. Intro: state the problem bluntly, then talk to the reader as an equal
2. Set expectations: "this is long, save it, grab a notebook". The warning stops people quitting halfway
3. Body: the big ideas and why things happen
4. Ending: a protocol, meaning step-by-step actions the reader can follow

#### How it was made

80% of writing is research and planning the outline. If you're staring at a blank page, you haven't done enough research

### Mistakes

- writing an article from a blank page without research
- giving tips instead of a system someone can follow
- a vague promise with no time frame

### Try it

List 7 of your posts that did well. Ask whether they could be the sections of one article, and write the one promise that holds them together

## Lesson 8.16: From views to money: the attention conversion funnel

### Big idea

Views only turn into money if four things all work, and they multiply. If any one of them is zero, the result is zero

### How it works

#### The four steps

The attention conversion funnel is how attention turns into sales. Its four steps multiply, so one zero means zero

1. A bullseye signal: in the first lines, the viewer thinks "that's exactly my problem". Name their pain and the before and after
2. A trust bank: every useful post is a deposit. Give, give, give, then ask
3. A clear ramp: every piece needs a way out, like a lead magnet (a free download that matches that video's topic), a sales page or a DM
4. An offer that fits: the buyer does quick math on whether they will get the result for this price. A good guide is that they should get 10 times the price back within 30 days

#### Why the ramp must match

Imagine talking about New York for 8 hours, then offering an exit to Tulsa. It doesn't work, because the exit doesn't match where the person was going

Your content and your ramp have to point the same way

#### What builds trust

From weakest to strongest: a study, a stranger's testimonial, your own result, several named client results, showing your skill in your content, and a warm recommendation from someone they already trust

A warm recommendation is the strongest, which is why shares are so important

### Mistakes

- making a great video with no ramp
- using a lead magnet that doesn't match that video's topic
- treating the four steps as extras when they multiply

### Try it

Take one post. Mark whether each of the 4 steps is there: bullseye signal, trust, ramp, offer. Fix the weakest one first

## Lesson 8.17: An audience of buyers

### Big idea

Viewers are interested, but buyers are trying to solve a problem. Build for the buyer's pain, and measure dollars, not views

### How it works

#### What a buyer needs

Viewers are just interested. Buyers are trying to solve something, and they need three things

- a pain point (a problem that hurts)
- a solution from you that is better, faster or cheaper than what they have now
- a reason to trust you

Views are not the goal, dollars are. A small niche gives small views but can still bring you the same 15 clients

#### 6 steps to build a buyer audience

1. Define the buyer
2. Map their whole workflow, step by step
3. Pick the most painful step
4. Write down what they do about it today. This is what you have to beat
5. Find your less obvious, faster way, because this is where the value is
6. Package it into something they can buy

#### What keeps you from buyers

An entertainment channel can have millions of followers and still sell very little. Views from entertainment don't make buyers

Topics that are too broad or all over the place, the same advice as everyone else, and no clear next step also keep buyers away

### Mistakes

- running an entertainment channel when you want buyers
- topics too broad or all over the place
- giving the same advice as everyone
- posting without a next step for the viewer

### Try it

Write your buyer's workflow in steps. Circle the most painful step, then write what they do about it today and one less obvious way to solve it

## Lesson 8.18: Content that sells: the \$10,000 test and how often to promote

### Big idea

Chasing millions of views brings beginners with no money. A specific audience that has money gives fewer views and more cash, and you need to ask for the sale often enough

### How it works

#### The \$10,000 test

A specific audience that has money means fewer views but much more cash. Before posting, ask: would someone pay \$10,000 for this information? If not, it's entertainment, and entertainment rarely sells

Content that proves results sells best. "Build your personal brand" is forgettable, while "how I grew my X from 0 to 214,000 in 12 months with 3 formats" is proof

Use at most 3 formats you can repeat. Change what goes in, not the structure

#### Get ideas from your buyers

Ask your buyers a 3-question survey:

1. What is your biggest goal this year?
2. What keeps you up at night?
3. If you had a magic wand, what would you want my help with?

#### A product ladder

The ladder is: free content, then a \$0 to \$50 product (a guide or template), then \$50 to \$500, then \$500 to \$5,000, then a high-priced offer

Start with free content plus one paid step. Add the next step only when the one below is full of customers

#### How often to promote

Products don't sell themselves. You must send people to them on purpose, and the odds that someone clicks your bio link and buys are tiny

At 7 posts a week, one promo is 14% of your content. At 90 posts a month with 4 promos, it's about 4%. More content gives more chances to sell without being annoying

Be clear on what you sell, what to do and how to buy. Put a CTA (the one thing you want people to do next) in some posts

#### Paid boosts

Don't boost posts from an organic account that works. Paid viewers blur who your audience is, and your reach can drop

Boost the winning post from a separate ad account instead

### Mistakes

- chasing the biggest audience instead of the right one
- promoting only once a week
- boosting posts from the organic account that works
- launching a whole product ladder at once

### Try it

Run the \$10,000 test on your last 5 posts. Mark each one as worth paying for or entertainment

## Lesson 8.19: Content campaigns and the funnel by audience stage

### Big idea

Stop thinking one post at a time. Pick one story to tell, give it a month, and match each piece to where your audience is on their journey

### How it works

#### Campaigns, not single posts

Pick one main story you want people to believe and build on it for a month. Pieces can look different and still point the same way

Say your story is that small teams do better than big ones. You post a hiring breakdown, a tutorial, an article, a case study and a meme, and every one supports the same conclusion

Being consistent doesn't mean repeating one sentence 100 times. It means 100 different pieces of evidence pointing to one conclusion. Repeat the cycle with new evidence, because most people didn't see it all

#### The funnel by audience stage

Not everyone is at the same stage. If someone thinks their process is fine, a 20-minute tutorial won't change their mind

- top of the funnel: awareness. Reach new people and introduce the problem
- middle: belief and trust. Show your thinking, proof, process and results
- bottom: people buy. Show the offer, who it's for, testimonials and the next step

How much of each you need depends on what you sell. A broad personal brand needs more top. A high-priced offer needs more middle and bottom

#### Find where your audience gets stuck

If they read but don't follow you, your positioning (what you are known for) or promise is weak

If they follow but never use anything, you're not practical enough. If they use your ideas but don't know what you sell, you need a bridge to your offer

Find the gap first, then make the next piece

### Mistakes

- repeating the same sentence 100 times
- stopping your message right when the market starts to notice it
- making a tutorial for someone who doesn't yet think they need it

### Try it

Name one story to run for a month. List 4 different pieces of evidence that point to it, one for each stage of the funnel plus one extra

## Lesson 8.20: Launches and creator collabs

### Big idea

A launch works when the right people understand your product, your team is ready to answer questions, and you have a plan for what comes next

### How it works

#### Find the right people

Look for people who deal with the problem every day, specialists who can explain what your product changes, and customers who can show results

Read their posts and replies first. Who pays attention to them, and would that audience try your product?

You should be able to say why that audience would care

#### The creator brief

When you ask creators to share your product, send a short brief (a note with the key facts): what the product does, what they can test themselves, and what evidence backs up your claim

Ask for their honest view after trying it. Make paid partnerships clear, and let them use their own words, because if everyone posts the same paragraph you lose the benefit

#### Handle the response

Agree who answers product questions and who handles business offers, and answer where people commented

Keep a shared doc of questions that come up again and again, objections (reasons people hesitate) and unexpected uses. Repeated questions become your FAQ (a list of common questions with answers)

Share the strongest contributions, with permission and credit. A customer demo can become your next post

#### Plan the next wave

Check which posts brought the right people. Look past views to trials, relevant DMs and sales calls

Follow up with the creators who brought useful conversations, and give them something new, like a customer story or an update that answers the questions from their first post

### Mistakes

- everyone posting the same paragraph
- sending a generic DM after someone liked a post
- ignoring questions that keep coming back
- counting only views, not trials and sales calls

### Try it

Write a 5-line creator brief for a product you know. Then say which of the three kinds of people (daily users, specialists, customers with results) you would approach first

## Lesson 8.21: Growth phases for a company, and founders with no audience

### Big idea

A company's social growth moves in phases, from content led by the founder to content led by the brand. A founder with no audience starts small, with one platform and by showing the real process

### How it works

#### Three phases of scaling content

These phases come from one creator who works with startups, so treat the numbers as rough guides, not rules

Phase 1 (0 to 1 million views a month) is founder-led content. Find a face for the brand and post 1 to 2 launch videos or posts a week

People prefer a personal page over a brand page

Phase 2 (1 to 10 million) is growing the channels that already win. You hire a copywriter who finds winning angles on one platform and rewrites them for others, a short-form editor, and a creative director for launches

Phase 3 (10 to 100 million) moves from founder-led to brand-led, with a team of 10 to 20 split into small groups that each focus on one channel. This phase needs money or deep talent, often both

#### Founders with no audience

Make content about the problem you solve, not your product. Spend at least 60 minutes a day on it

Post the real process: what broke, what you learned, what you're testing. Nobody cares about launch updates

Start with one platform, ideally X or YouTube. Add short video after 2 to 3 months by cutting it from your long-form content

Split your time 50/50 between building and getting seen. Most founders are at 95/5, and should show their face, because nobody connects with a cartoon profile picture

#### Small creators and big creators

What gets you to your first \$10,000 a month isn't what gets you to \$100,000. Small creators who only post for their offer can get stuck closing sales every day

Make content for the sale, and also add some broader content to grow, because wide attention opens doors like investors and advisory roles

### Mistakes

- posting launch updates nobody cares about
- splitting your time 95/5 between building and getting seen
- hiding behind a logo with no face

### Try it

If you work on a company, write down which of the three phases it is in and what the next hire or workflow would be

## Lesson 8.22: Go to market: pick channels by how you sell

### Big idea

Go to market means working out how you will reach and sell to buyers, and it comes first. If the offer is unclear, channels just spread the unclear version faster

### How it works

#### Start with the offer

Write down what you sell and what counts as a win, such as a signup, a booked call or a purchase

How you sell picks your channels. A product people can try for free needs content, SEO (getting found on Google) and a great first experience

A product sold on a call needs outbound (reaching out to people first), ads and direct outreach

#### Research, then pick two or three

Look at the competitors that buyers compare you with: their pages, prices and repeated claims. Note the doubts and words buyers use in comments

- Pick two or three channels where buyers already spend time and forget the rest for now
- Judge every channel on sales and signups, never views

#### Landing pages and lead capture

Keep it small: one page that sells one thing to one kind of buyer

After signup, speed is everything. The lead lands in a CRM (a tool that stores your contacts) or a sheet, a welcome email goes out right away, and follow-ups take over

#### Add channels one at a time

Start with the first channel, and add the next only when the last one passes this test: did it do its job this morning without me asking?

### Examples

A playbook for courses:

Start with X and long-form YouTube. Every winning post gets a freebie that grows the email list

The paid product comes after the free one

Why it works: How you sell decides the order

### Mistakes

- an unclear offer pushed through more channels
- picking channels by views instead of sales and signups
- adding a new channel before the last one works

### Try it

Write one line: what you sell and what counts as a win. Then name two channels where buyers already spend time

## Lesson 8.23: Outbound email, SEO and ads

### Big idea

Each of these channels only works after your page and emails turn visitors into customers. Keep it small, track one thing, and keep people in charge of budget and plans

### How it works

#### Outbound email

Outbound email means emailing people who haven't asked to hear from you. You find prospects (people who might buy), send a short series of emails, and track replies in a CRM (a tool that stores your contacts)

A first line gets read when you write right after something happened at their company, like raising money or hiring a new head of marketing. Find the reason, note it, and write the first line about them

Every cold email needs your real address and a working unsubscribe

#### Making emails reach the inbox

This is called deliverability. Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC on every sending domain (three technical settings that prove your email is real)

Warm up new inboxes for at least 2 weeks, and send about 30 emails a day per inbox, increasing slowly

Stop following up the moment someone replies or the email bounces (comes back undelivered)

#### SEO

SEO means making your pages appear when people search. Find one page where ranking higher would make you money

Check four things:

1. Can Google find and read the page?
2. Does it answer what people searched for?
3. Is it better than what ranks above it?
4. Does it push people toward buying or signing up?

Then change one thing on one page, wait a week and compare. It's slow, but it's the only way to know what worked

#### Ads

Start ads last. Paying for visitors to a page that doesn't sell loses money faster

If an AI assistant helps run your ads, new campaigns start paused, it can pause a loser but never raise a budget, and budget stays with a person

Don't use fake reviews or a fake person praising your product. Platforms can shut accounts without warning

### Mistakes

- whole-site SEO audits with no clear order
- sending 30 or more emails a day from a cold inbox
- letting an AI assistant raise ad budgets
- fake reviews or a fake person praising the product

### Try it

Pick one page that could make money. Write down the 4 SEO checks and the one change you would test for a week

- posting alone isn't growth. Get people to see your work

- start with 1 to 2 platforms and master one first
- reply with your own experience, not a repeat of the original post
- quote a post to add something new, not to say "great post"
- credit and tag the people whose ideas you use
- repost your winners every 60 days or so, and go all in when something takes off
- post steadily and judge only after enough practice (one creator committed to 100 pieces)
- pick a sub-niche and commit to it for about a year
- write your bio for the reader: "I write about X for Y"
- copy what makes a viral post work, never its content
- check every piece for four things: it speaks to the right person, it builds trust, it has a next step, and it has an offer
- before posting, ask: would someone pay \$10,000 for this?
- never boost posts from the organic account that works
- judge channels on sales and signups, never views
