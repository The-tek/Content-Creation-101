## 5.1 What makes an idea go viral

*8 min read*

> **The big idea:** A viral idea (one that gets shared far beyond your followers) passes three quick tests in the viewer's head: do I care, is it new, am I curious. It also works for fans, casual viewers and strangers at the same time

### The three questions every viewer asks

Without noticing it, every viewer asks three things about your idea. Do I care? Is this new to me? Am I curious to see where it goes?

If any answer is no, they leave. A topic people care about but have heard a hundred times fails the second question. A brand new topic nobody cares about fails the first

An old topic can feel new with a fresh angle, which is a different way of looking at it

### Three kinds of viewers

Core viewers know, like and trust you, and they buy from you. Casual viewers know you a little and watch now and then. New viewers have never seen you, and this is where growth comes from

A strong idea works for all three at once. Say a video is called "Meetings are the biggest time waster in any company". Business owners agree with it, employees stuck in pointless meetings share it, and everyone knows what a meeting is

To do this, start simple so anyone can follow. Then go deeper later in the video with details only your core fans need

### How to check an idea

A viral idea usually has these traits:

- it works for your fans, casual viewers and strangers
- it has something new, so the viewer learns something they haven't heard before
- you can explain it in a title and a thumbnail. If you can't, you have an interesting thing, not yet a good idea
- people get excited when you mention it. Tell a few friends or people in your niche, and if they don't look excited, change it

### Any niche can do this

Take woodworking. "Reviewing a tool" is a boring video. "99% of woodworkers get this wrong" is the same niche with a much stronger framing, which means the way the idea is presented

Worried your niche is too small? Search it on YouTube, filter for long videos and sort by most viewed. The top results show how big your niche can get

Start in your niche, then move to the nearby niches that overlap with it. If you go broad on day one, you lose your first fans

**Example: A narrow topic that anyone clicks**

A lecturer in a very narrow field of plant growing got about 12 million views with the title "How the UN is holding back the Sahara Desert"

*Why it works:* The topic is narrow, but the title is not. Everyone knows the UN, and a desert being held back is a strong picture, so a stranger can click without knowing anything about plants

### Mistakes to avoid

- keeping an idea you can't put into a title and a thumbnail, and calling it a great idea
- making content only your core fans understand, which doesn't help you grow
- going broad before you have a small group of fans
- saying your niche is too small without checking the most viewed videos in it

### Where creators disagree

Some creators say you should stay in one niche. Others say you should cover every problem your ideal customer has. They agree more than it looks: both want an idea from your niche that is framed so strangers also click

### Try it

Take your last 5 content ideas. For each one, write one line on how a core fan, a casual viewer and a total stranger would react to the title. Rewrite the idea with the weakest reaction so a stranger would want to click

## 5.2 Topic is king

*8 min read*

> **The big idea:** The topic you pick decides more than your hook, editing or camera. Choose it first, and test every topic with three questions: do enough people want it, does it fit your audience, and do you care about it

### Why the topic comes first

You can't fix a bad topic with good editing. Great lighting, gear and editing can't save a subject nobody cares about

From most to least important: first the topic, then the packaging (the angle, the format, the title and the thumbnail), then everything else like camera, lighting and editing

If you ignored all other advice and only picked the best topics, you would already be ahead of most people

### Question 1: demand

Demand means how many people care. Say two videos have the same editing, gear and hook

One is "get abs in 90 days", the other is "build a B2B software company in 90 days". The abs video wins, because far more people want abs

To judge demand, ask who your audience is, what they want to see, and what kinds of videos already do well in your niche. The top accounts in your niche answer all three

Let data pick the topic, then add your own opinion. Find topics people already proved they want in the best posts of top accounts

Then ask what you believe about that topic that most people get wrong

### Questions 2 and 3: fit and interest

Fit means your audience comes to you for this topic. A fitness video on a branding account would flop, even though fitness has big demand

Interest means you genuinely care about the topic. If not, skip it, because viewers can feel when you don't care

Aim narrow, all the way down to your ideal viewer. Broad topics get views, but they don't build a small audience that loves your content

### Pick what people need

Build topics around problems your viewer already feels, not around trends. "3 trends in skincare" is nice to have

"If you struggle with acne, try these 3 things" is something people need

The most common mistake is selfish content: talking about yourself instead of what the viewer gets

Evergreen content is content someone can find years later and still find useful, unlike news. A library of it pays off over time

**Three questions for a topic**

1. Demand: do enough people care?
2. Fit: does my audience come to me for this?
3. Interest: do I care enough to keep making it?

### Mistakes to avoid

- trying to fix a weak topic with better editing, lighting or gear
- starting from your own point of view and hoping the topic turns out to be popular
- chasing demand outside what your audience comes to you for
- picking topics you don't care about

### Try it

List 10 topics you could make content about. Score each from 1 to 5 on demand, fit and interest, and cut anything that scores 1 on any of the three

For your top 3, name a problem your viewer already feels

## 5.3 Where good ideas come from

*9 min read*

> **The big idea:** Good ideas come from a system, not from staring at a blank page. Collect ideas all day, listen to your customers and comments, and set time aside regularly to sort what you collected

### Build an idea engine

An idea engine is a simple system that gives you ideas on a regular basis. Everything you regularly read, listen to or watch (newsletters, podcasts, videos) is your source

Whenever something makes you think "hmm" or "oh wow", save it on a board, for example a Notion page. When you want to write, pick from the board

Write an idea down the moment it comes, when it is both likely to do well and exciting to you. Ideas often come on a walk, while reading a real book, or in a conversation

### Six types of ideas

For every subtopic you cover, try each of these types:

- pain points: what frustrates your audience or keeps them stuck. People feel understood when you name these
- how-tos: step-by-step methods they can try today
- lessons you learned: mistakes you made and what you'd tell a beginner
- why it's important: why they should care and what ignoring it costs them
- harsh truths: what nobody in your niche wants to say. These stand out from all the other posts
- your opinion on popular ideas: common advice you disagree with

### Ask your customers and read your comments

Ask real customers about their main problems, goals and biggest dreams. Their answers give you ideas that solve exactly what they struggle with

A simple survey has three questions: what is your biggest goal this year? What keeps you up at night? If you had a magic wand, what would you want my help with?

Then read the comments under top posts in your niche. A question nobody answered, like "But how do I do this with no money?", is your next video

### Use your own day

Everything in your day can be an idea: a question someone asked that you had to explain, something you watched or read, a thought at the gym. Aim for 3 to 5 a day

For each one, ask whether you have a strong opinion, a story, or something you can explain so people relate

Collect 100 ideas, then pick the one that fits your three topic questions best. If you use AI to help, tell it the shape of hook you want, like "is it possible to...". Without a shape, it gives you flat statements

**Example: Whiteboard sessions**

Customers told a creator that their favourite content was watching him help other founders live. So he started a regular series where he sits down with a founder and works through their problem

*Why it works:* The idea came from asking customers, not from guessing

### Mistakes to avoid

- only reading light social posts instead of books and deep content a bit above your level
- writing an idea down later instead of the moment it comes
- describing problems in your words instead of your audience's
- asking AI for ideas without telling it the shape of hook you want

### Try it

Pick one subtopic you know well and write one idea for each of the six types. Then open the comments under a big post in your niche and copy 3 unanswered questions word for word

## 5.4 What an idea is made of: topic, angle, format

*7 min read*

> **The big idea:** An idea is more than a topic. Before you write anything, check three parts: the topic, the angle (your point of view on it) and the format (how you present it). You can swap each part to turn one topic into many ideas

### Topic, angle and format

A great topic with the wrong angle or format still flops, so check all three before you write a script

The topic is a conversation that already exists. That is easier than starting a new one

To check that people care, look at your own sales calls, comments and past posts, or at other people's posts, Reddit and Google search trends

The angle is your point of view on the topic. Choose the one you agree with most and that you think the most people will connect with

### Format

The format is how you present the idea so the viewer understands it quickly, for example a ranking, a list or a before and after

A ranking works because the viewer can see where you put each thing

### Many ideas from one topic

Swap the angle and the format, and one topic turns into many ideas. A simple rule is 1 popular topic x 3 angles x 3 formats = 9 videos

The angle also includes the key facts you pick. "Apple's logo history" and "Apple's 2004 ad campaign" share a topic but are different videos

Once you have a topic, you can ask an AI to list about 50 angles, then pick one

### Common knowledge, better packaged

You don't need secret information. You need a better package, with one idea per post and numbers, results and specific details

Top brands say the same 5 or 6 things over and over. You can repeat yourself, as long as each version is packaged better

**Example: One topic, two angles**

Say the topic is meetings. "Are meetings a good use of time?" is a flat angle

"Meetings are the biggest time waster in any company" takes a side

*Why it works:* Same topic, very different result. The strong angle takes a side that people react to

### Mistakes to avoid

- writing a script before the topic, angle and format are checked
- changing the angle too much when you borrow an idea that already worked
- thinking you need secret information instead of a better package

### Try it

Pick one topic. Ask an AI for 20 angles on it, then choose the 3 you agree with most

Pair each with 3 formats (a list, a ranking, a story, a how-to). You now have 9 ideas from one topic

## 5.5 Making old topics feel new

*9 min read*

> **The big idea:** Almost every topic has been covered. You make an old topic feel new by mixing it with a different field, giving it a fresh name or angle, and taking a side that goes against what people expect

### Mix two worlds that don't usually meet

Much of creativity is joining two fields that don't normally meet. Spend time outside your niche, see what works there, then combine it with a method you already know

For example, "dopamine ladders" mixes psychology with storytelling

The same news can also look new through a different lens, which is your own way of looking at the story. When a famous singer went to the Super Bowl, most creators talked about her outfit. One covered what her visit did for the NFL's business, and nobody else was making that video

### 4 ways to make an old topic new

- a new frame: look at the topic from a different side, like the business side of a celebrity story
- a new name: give a known idea a name of its own, like "dopamine ladder". A named idea feels like a discovery
- a new update: say what changed recently in an old topic
- a new finding: share something you tested or discovered yourself

### The illusion of novelty

The illusion of novelty means an idea feels new to the viewer even if it is old. To create it, start with an old topic, add a fresh angle and tie it to a result the viewer wants. For example, "drinking water at the right time gives you more energy than your morning coffee"

Then add proof. The strongest proof is someone exactly like the viewer who got the result. A study is weaker, because people wonder if it applies to them

Don't weaken your idea by saying "this has been known for decades". Share it like something not many people know yet

### Contrarian takes

A contrarian take is a belief that goes against what most people in your niche say. Write down 3 to 5 of them

Then follow these steps: pick a subtopic, state the common belief, share your different view, and explain why your view is right

A strong belief you hold does more for a brand than titles, thumbnails or hooks

### Mistakes to avoid

- recycling the same ideas as everyone else, which can get views but not loyal fans
- using a random comparison instead of a true opposite when you contrast
- weakening your own idea by saying it's old news

### Try it

Write down one common belief in your niche. Then write your different view and three reasons it's right. Finally, give the idea a name of 2 to 4 words, the way "dopamine ladder" was named

## 5.6 Copy the format, not the content

*9 min read*

> **The big idea:** The best creators learn from what already works. Borrow the format, the topic or the shape of the hook, but never the words or someone else's point of view. Then fill it with your own stories

### Learn from others before you invent

Musicians play other people's songs before they write their own. In the same way, look at what works for other people and what worked for you before, and find which topics, formats and angles do well

Take inspiration from the topic, the angle and the format. Never take the words or the other person's point of view. Then add your own stories and experience so it becomes yours

Study how big creators grew, not where they are today. Look at what they posted 2 to 4 years ago, while they were growing fast

### Copy the format

A format is the shape of a post: a ranking, a list, a before and after, a thread, a carousel. Look at formats in nearby niches and bring them into yours

The best move is to copy a format that is going viral right now, never the content, and change it to fit your niche. If a format keeps appearing in your feed, there is a reason

The format is more important than the niche, so a productivity creator can learn from fitness videos that use the same format

### Scroll like a creator

When a video hooks you, ask why, ask who it was made for, and read the comments. Then bring the idea into your niche

Keep a swipe file (a folder of saved examples) of what stops you from scrolling. When you save a video, break it down with these questions:

1. Why did it work?
2. How does the hook grab people in the first seconds?
3. How fast does it move?
4. What is the sound?
5. How can I adapt it to my brand and audience?

### Borrow what is already proven

One way is to open a video with a clip that already went viral, then teach something about it. The hook has already proved it grabs people

A small account can also show a brand or celebrity people already know and build on their fame

### Warnings

Never copy someone one for one, and don't react to everything other creators do

Just because something went viral doesn't make it true

If everyone copies the same look, viewers get used to it and it stops working. Build your own look

**Example: Adding your own opinion to a common topic**

One creator took the very common topic "how to get more views and followers" and added a method for picking topics that will do well

*Why it works:* The topic was proven. The opinion was new, because nobody else was saying it

### Mistakes to avoid

- copying the words or someone else's point of view instead of the format
- copying what big creators post today instead of what they posted while growing
- assuming something is true because it went viral

### Try it

Save 5 posts or videos that stopped you from scrolling this week, at least 2 from outside your niche. Break each one down with the five questions. Then write one idea for your own niche from the best one

## 5.7 Finding outliers

*8 min read*

> **The big idea:** An outlier is a post that did way better than that account usually does. Outliers show you what people want right now, so you don't have to guess what to make

### What an outlier is

Every account has a normal. Say a creator's videos usually get about 10,000 views. Then one gets 100,000. That video is an outlier

It didn't happen by luck. Something about the topic, the title or the thumbnail made far more people click. If you work out what it was, you can use the same idea in your own way

Most creators use a simple rule: a post is an outlier when it gets at least 3 times the account's usual views. On X, look for posts with at least 2 times the usual engagement

### Where to look

- the last 6 months only, because what people want changes fast
- smaller accounts first. One video with 250,000 views on a channel with 20,000 subscribers tells you more than a big creator's video, because on a big channel everything gets a lot of views
- other niches too. "I tested 100 years of kids' toys" can become "I tested 100 years of morning routines"

Tools like ViewStats can sort videos by how far above their channel's average they did, so you don't have to work it out by hand

### What usually makes an outlier

Outliers almost always come from the idea, the title or the thumbnail. A better camera or nicer colours help a little, but they won't get you 10 times more views

So when you find one, ask yourself 3 questions:

1. What is the topic, and why do so many people care about it?
2. What does the title promise?
3. What does the thumbnail or the first line show?

Write your answers down. After 10 or 20 outliers you start to see the same patterns again and again

### How to study outliers on X (the anomaly exercise)

This exercise trains you to write posts people stop scrolling for

1. Pick 5 to 10 creators whose writing you like
2. Spend 1 hour a day for a week going through their posts
3. Save every post that got at least 2 times their usual engagement
4. Keep going until you have 20 to 30 of them
5. Under each one, write 3 to 5 short notes on why it worked: the hook, the feeling it gives, what people learn

You can also paste a post into ChatGPT or Claude and ask it to explain why it worked and how to write your own version

### Mistakes to avoid

- copying the outlier word for word instead of using the idea in your own way
- studying big accounts, where every post gets a lot of views
- looking at posts from years ago, when people wanted different things

### Try it

Pick 3 creators in your niche. Find one outlier from each of them in the last 6 months. For each one, write down the topic, what the title promises and what the first line or thumbnail shows. Then write one post idea that uses the same topic in your own way

## 5.8 Breaking down outliers

*7 min read*

> **The big idea:** Finding an outlier is half the job. The other half is working out why it won and what you can reuse. Ask a few questions about it, split it into its 7 parts, keep the parts that keep winning and change the rest

### Questions to ask about an outlier

Save any successful video into a doc or Notion page and write down your answers to these questions:

1. What is unique about it: the topic, the thumbnail?
2. Was its packaging (the title, the thumbnail and the format) different from the rest of the niche?
3. Why did this one go viral when similar ones didn't?

Don't brush it off with "they have more money" or "they're bigger". Write down what you think made it work

Remember that you only see the winners. The creator may have tried the same video eight other times before it worked

### The 7 parts of an idea

You can split any idea into 7 parts: topic, angle, hook, story structure (the order you tell it in), visual format (how it looks, like a list or a screen recording), key visuals (the main pictures) and audio

Think of them as Lego bricks. You can keep some and change others

### Keep 2 parts, change 5

1. Find outliers
2. Split each one into its 7 parts and look for patterns across many winners
3. Keep 2 parts that keep winning and change the other 5

This is like swapping cards in poker: you keep the good ones and swap the rest

Study the same type of content. Short video should study short video. A tweet's topic can inspire a video, but its hook and structure won't work the same way

### Learn from other people's tests

When you read how others tested their titles and thumbnails, you save yourself from running hundreds of tests of your own

Imagine a travel creator who tested three thumbnails for one video. The one that split the picture between economy and first class won, and a similar video from another creator a year earlier had the same result. Checking that first would have saved the test

### Mistakes to avoid

- brushing off a competitor's outlier as luck or a big budget
- forgetting that you only see the videos that worked, not the ones that failed
- borrowing a hook or structure from a different type of content
- changing every part at once instead of keeping the ones that win

### Try it

Take 3 outliers you found. Fill in the 7 parts for each in a table and circle any part that repeats in at least 2 of them. Plan one piece that keeps those parts and changes the rest

## 5.9 Choosing which ideas to make

*8 min read*

> **The big idea:** The best creators aren't more creative, they drop bad ideas faster. Collect many ideas, cut most of them with a few simple tests, and make only the best few. Check early that the idea has something clear to show in the first seconds

### Drop bad ideas faster

Many people find one outlier and remake it. It's better to collect 20 to 30 ideas and drop 25 of them

Say you bring 12 ideas and make 4. The other 8 weren't wasted, because they helped you pick the best 4

Describe every idea in 3 parts: topic (AI tools), angle (these AI tools will make you money), and format (a tier list ranked by how much money each one makes)

Keep coming up with ideas separate from making them. The person who makes the content tends to pick the easiest idea, not the one that will do best. Split it by person, or by time: ideas from this week get made two weeks from now

### The idea funnel

A funnel cuts many ideas down to a few:

1. Start with 100 ideas
2. Cut the ones that don't work for your core fans, casual viewers and strangers. About 40 are left
3. Ask: can I write the title and sketch the thumbnail? About 20 are left
4. Ask: am I excited to make it? About 10 are left
5. Write a one-page plan for each, with a rough thumbnail, a title and 1 to 3 reasons it will work. Ask other people what they think, then make the best 3 or 4

### Pick by what the viewer will see

When an idea comes to you, first ask what you will show in the first 3 to 5 seconds. If there is no good picture, change the idea or create a picture with animated graphics or stock footage

Ideas you can see do better than ideas you can only describe. A video about an abstract "generative world model" got little attention. A video about life-size floor plans, where you can walk through your home design before you build it, got millions, because the thing was right there on screen

Even teaching posts need a picture. An expert fills a jar with golf balls, then rocks, then sand, and it all fits. In the other order it doesn't, so do the big tasks first

### Final checks

Run every idea through five questions:

- is it relevant to your ideal viewer?
- is it a small effort for a big reward?
- is it something most people haven't heard?
- is it proven?
- can you actually do it?

### Mistakes to avoid

- remaking one outlier instead of finding 20 to 30 ideas and dropping most
- letting the person who makes the content also choose the ideas, so the easiest ones win
- picking ideas with nothing to show in the first seconds
- spending a week on a title instead of an hour

### Try it

Write 20 ideas in 10 minutes. Then, in a separate session, run them through the funnel and write a one-page plan for the last 3

## 5.10 Why packaging decides the result

*8 min read*

> **The big idea:** Packaging is how an idea looks before anyone watches or reads it: the title, the thumbnail and the first lines. The same idea can get 5 to 10 times more views with better packaging, so it deserves far more of your time than most people give it

### What packaging is

If people don't click, they don't watch. That is why packaging is so important

On YouTube, packaging is the title, the thumbnail and the first 30 to 60 seconds. On text platforms it is the first line of a post and how the post looks

Good packaging makes people curious, promises a clear payoff (what the viewer gets), is specific, and fits the platform

### Same idea, very different results

One creator's idea can flop while another creator takes the exact same idea, packages it differently and gets 5 to 10 times the results. Say one gets 10,000 views and the other gets 100,000

Often the content isn't the problem, the packaging is. One team's old YouTube videos had great information but unclear packaging, and results improved once they changed it

A title that is 20% better can mean double, triple or even far more views, because YouTube shows it to more and more people

### How much time packaging deserves

Most people spend 95% of their time on content and 5% on packaging. A better aim is about half and half

The idea, title and thumbnail decide a large part of how a video does. The exact split is debatable, but the direction is clear: don't put 5% of your time into something that decides so much

Some channels make 10 or more thumbnails per video before they pick one

### How people choose what to click

- On YouTube, people glance at the thumbnail, read the title, look back at the thumbnail to understand it, and then click
- Your intro must confirm the click: repeat the title's promise in the first seconds

On text platforms, the first line is your title. Write it like one, and rewrite it yourself if an AI gave it to you, because its first line is almost always weak

### Mistakes to avoid

- spending 95% of your time on content and 5% on packaging
- choosing a weaker title because it sounds more like your brand
- keeping the first line the AI gave you
- an intro that doesn't confirm the title's promise

### Try it

Take one post or video that did badly. Without changing the content, write 5 new titles or first lines

Pick the strongest by asking: does it make people curious, promise a clear payoff, stay specific, and fit the platform?

## 5.11 Thumbnails: simple, clear, one click

*9 min read*

> **The big idea:** A thumbnail has one job: to get the click. Show fewer things, show a clear emotion, and make sure people understand it in a split second

### Keep it simple

The less a thumbnail shows, the better. Use one to three things to look at. A face, another face, an object, text, a logo and more text is too much

Beginners fill all the space, but many of the most viral thumbnails have empty space. One example is just an arrow, a saw and a face

A small exaggeration works well, as long as it isn't clickbait (a promise the video doesn't keep). Gold on the world's most expensive saw is a little exaggerated, but it doesn't mislead

### The glance test

Put the thumbnail small on a slide, with a blank white slide before and after it. Flick onto it quickly and ask: can I understand it in a split second?

Many creators make packaging that needs 5 seconds to read, which is far more time than viewers give it. If it fails the test, keep simplifying

### A thumbnail recipe

1. A big face with a clear emotion. People are drawn to eyes, and a face helps people recognise you
2. Short text of 3 to 5 words. People scan, they don't read
3. Optional: one picture that backs up the idea, such as money flying around

The emotion must match the title. A title like "Stop wasting your life" needs a person who clearly looks upset, and a big smile feels wrong

Never repeat the title on the thumbnail. You get two chances to make the viewer feel something, so don't waste one

### Look different from your niche

Study the thumbnails in your own niche, then do the opposite. If everyone's thumbnails are dark and busy, go light and minimal. The brain notices differences in colour and brightness before it reads anything

Also check your thumbnail in dark mode, because many viewers use it

### Test your thumbnails

YouTube lets you test several titles and thumbnails against each other (an A/B/C test). Use it to check which one gets more clicks

The click rate (CTR) is the share of people who see a video and click it. In the first hour and first 24 hours it is a strong sign of how the video will do

**Example: The saw thumbnail**

An arrow, a saw and a face, with gold on the world's most expensive saw. The rest of the thumbnail is empty

*Why it works:* Three things to look at, a small exaggeration, and no lie

### Mistakes to avoid

- filling every inch of the thumbnail with a face, another face, an object, text and a logo
- a big smile on a negative title
- repeating the title word for word on the thumbnail
- spending too long on design instead of showing one clear thing

### Where creators disagree

Some creators say to keep text to 3 to 5 words because people scan and don't read. Others say that in some countries thumbnails with more text get more clicks. Both agree you should study the thumbnails in your own niche first

### Try it

Take one thumbnail and run the glance test: put it small between two blank slides and flick onto it. If you can't say what it is in a split second, cut things until you can

## 5.12 Titles that get the click

*9 min read*

> **The big idea:** A good title makes one clear promise about something people want. Be clear before you are clever, and use four emotions (hope, dream, fear, blocker) to package any topic in different ways

### Be clear before you are clever

Ask yourself: what is the smallest promise I can make that still gets people interested? Put that in the title and thumbnail

The test: if a stranger can't say what the video is about within 3 seconds of seeing the thumbnail, it is too confusing. People scroll, they don't wait to figure it out

A title that got over 20 million views is simple: "World's fastest camera drone vs F1 car". You understand it instantly

### Add a feeling and a promise

The same topic can be packaged differently. "Here's how to grow a business" is plain. "Here's how to grow a business so fast it feels like cheating" is stronger, because it adds a feeling and a promise

A title format that has worked in many niches is "If I had to start [X] over again, here's what I'd do"

### Hope, dream, fear, blocker

One topic, like "how to make money", can be packaged four ways, depending on the emotion you aim at:

- hope, the short-term win: "how to make a million dollars in 2026"
- dream, the life they want in the long run: "if I wanted to achieve financial freedom in the next 12 months, I'd do this"
- fear, what they dread: "you'll never become rich until you understand this"
- blocker, what they think is stopping them: "how to get rich without talent, luck, or a trust fund"

Learn this method instead of copying 100 titles. Then you can write your own titles for any topic

### Title shapes that worked for long videos

These shapes did far better than their channel's usual numbers:

- "How to [big result] (Full 2026 Guide)"
- "[Topic] 101: A Guide to [big result]"
- "How to [skill] like the top 0.01%"
- proof in the title: "How we [big number] and made [result]"

They promise a big result everyone wants (money, freedom, being known) instead of a narrow skill, and the brackets promise the complete thing

### Mistakes to avoid

- a clever title nobody understands in 3 seconds
- a promise about a narrow skill when a big result everyone wants was available
- picking one emotion out of habit instead of testing hope, dream, fear and blocker

### Try it

Take one topic and write four titles: one each for hope, dream, fear and blocker. Cross out any that a stranger could not explain in 3 seconds, and keep the best one

## 5.13 Viral writing 101

*9 min read*

> **The big idea:** Viral posts are a craft. Decide which emotion you want the reader to feel, make every tweet make people want to read the next one, write in simple words, and check two numbers afterwards to see what worked

### Pick the emotion before you write

Keep a short list at the top of your document: LOL, WTF, "finally someone said it", aww, wow. Choose one before you write a word

The best case is one opinion that triggers two opposite emotions. Your fans think "finally someone said it" while the other side thinks "WTF". Both reactions spread the post

### Hook shapes you can reuse

- mythbuster: take something many people believe and show it is wrong
- "if you're out of the loop": explain the thing everyone keeps hearing about but doesn't understand
- an underdog story: a small person or team nobody expects to win against a giant
- "PSA": a short warning or tip that helps the reader
- "mistakes I made" or "what I wish I knew 5 years ago": you speak from lessons you learned, not as someone better than the reader

### Write simply

Write at a 2nd to 8th grade reading level. A phrase like "yield a positive ROI" makes nobody feel anything

Say two people share the same opinion. One writes it in simple words and the other in expert words. The simple one gets read and shared

### Check two numbers

After you post, divide likes by impressions (how many people saw it). 2 to 3% is a big hit

Then divide reposts by likes. 20 to 30% means the post will spread, because people repost what they want to be seen agreeing with

### Test small, then expand

Write each tweet so people want to read the next one. Write the hook 20 different ways before you pick one

A single tweet that does better than usual can become a thread (a series of connected tweets). The best tweet inside a thread can become its own post

Most people didn't see what you posted 6 months ago, so you can reuse what worked

### Mistakes to avoid

- writing before deciding which emotion the reader should feel
- using business words only experts know
- judging a post by likes alone and ignoring reposts per like

### Try it

Take one finished post and write the hook 20 different ways. Then check which emotion from the list the full post leaves behind

## 5.14 How the algorithm decides (and what creators disagree on)

*9 min read*

> **The big idea:** The algorithm (the platform's system that decides who sees a post) works like a matchmaker. It tests your post on a small group and shows it to more people if they enjoy it. This is a simplified model, not published platform rules

### The algorithm as a matchmaker

Platforms make money when people keep watching, so they try to match each video with the people who will enjoy it

First it works out who should like your video, from the visuals, the audio or text, the caption and data about you. Then it shows the video to a small test group, mostly people who don't follow you

If the test group enjoys it, the video is shown to a bigger group, then a bigger one. If results are bad, it stops. That is why a video can get stuck at around 200 views

### What the platform looks at

The main signs are how long people watch (or how much of the video they finish), and the engagement rate, which is likes plus comments plus shares divided by views

Short videos lose many viewers in the first 2 seconds, so a clear topic right away is important

### How to get more comments

- take a strong side, or the opposite side of what most people think
- exaggerate a little more, like "better than every pasta shop in the world"
- talk about things people already have opinions on. Naming Nike gets more replies than saying "shoes"
- make viewers feel something

### Replace "algorithm" with "audience"

Whenever you are about to say "algorithm", say "audience" instead, and you probably have the real answer

"The algorithm is punishing me" becomes "my audience isn't interested". "The algorithm didn't like my thumbnail change" becomes "my audience didn't click it"

Blaming the algorithm is an excuse. A good plan comes from understanding real people and predicting what they will do

### Mistakes to avoid

- treating the test group size as an official platform rule, because it is a simplified model
- chasing every claim that "the algorithm changed"
- relying on keywords and hashtags instead of making an interesting video

### Where creators disagree

Some creators say posting time, hashtags and captions don't make a difference. Others found that posting time did. Nobody outside the platforms knows for sure, so test it yourself

### Try it

Look at the last post you saw with lots of replies. Which of the ways to get more comments did it use?

## 5.15 The X algorithm: 16 rules

*8 min read*

> **The big idea:** One creator lists 16 rules for how the X algorithm works in 2026. They are his reading of the latest update, not official rules. The main idea is that you grow by understanding what the algorithm wants people to do with your content

### The main idea

You don't grow just by posting and replying more. The algorithm wants people to read, watch, reply, quote, share and follow

It is also getting better at telling real interest from likes and replies with no real interest behind them. Treat every rule below as "it appears that"

### What to make

- original posts, because they give you the best chance to reach people who don't follow you
- posts people want to share, since sharing is one of the strongest signs, especially when someone sends the link to a person
- posts people want to reply to or quote, like opinions, step-by-step methods and questions
- simple, honest videos you make yourself, where the first seconds are more important than ever
- one great post instead of six average ones, because your posts compete with each other in the same feed
- posts that earn the follow, by being useful, interesting or explaining something

### What to avoid

- clickbait, which means a title that promises more than the post gives. Keeping people's attention is what counts
- content people want to hide. "Not interested" is one of the strongest negative signs
- engagement bait such as "like if you agree", "comment yes to receive it" or "tag 3 friends"
- stuffing in hashtags
- repeating identical text, because it looks like spam. Reuse an idea with a new angle, format or example
- posting back to back, because posts compete for the same audience

### Timing and numbers

Small accounts (under 1,000 followers) appear to get extra reach, so it is the best time to try bold things

The time people spend on a post is the main number. Bookmarks appear to have little direct effect on reach

The first 48 hours are the most important. Reply to the comments on your own posts within the first 30 minutes, and don't expect an old post to come back to life

### Mistakes to avoid

- building your whole plan on replies and reposts
- posting six average posts instead of one great one
- using engagement bait or stuffing in hashtags
- waiting for the algorithm to bring a post back to life after 48 hours

### Where creators disagree

One creator says bookmarks have little direct effect on reach. Many others still see saves as a sign that a post was useful. Both can be true: a save can show value without making a post reach further

### Try it

Pick one of your recent posts and ask: would someone send it to a friend, reply to it, or follow you because of it?

## 5.16 The viral trap: chase the right views

*9 min read*

> **The big idea:** Not all views are equal. A video that goes viral with the wrong crowd can hurt everything you post after it. Aim for views from people who could actually become your customers

### Views from the wrong crowd

A huge viral video about a broad topic brings lots of views and few buyers. A specific video, like "mobility exercises for millennials", reaches the people who actually want it

Imagine a new page that posts a video for marketers and gets 50,000 views and 500 followers. Then it posts a funny gorilla video and gets 5 million views and 5,000 followers

Its next product video is shown to the gorilla followers, who came for fun. They don't react, so the algorithm stops showing it to people

### The idea rings

Think of four rings around the person you help. Say you do marketing for restaurant owners:

- core: their exact problem in their exact niche (marketing for restaurants)
- inner: their problem in a nearby niche (marketing for any local shop)
- outer: their problem in any niche (business marketing)
- max: anything about the broad topic (all marketing). Avoid this ring, because a viral video here brings the wrong followers

A good mix if you want to sell is 40% core, 40% inner, 20% outer and 0% max

### Three more checks

- does the post give one tip people can use, even if they never buy?
- would a restaurant owner send this post to another restaurant owner?
- are you staying on the same few topics for the same person? If you post about tech, then health, then politics, the algorithm mixes those audiences and your next video flops

### Be patient about who you gather

If you only aim for views, you get pulled toward topics that don't lead to what your business needs

Sometimes say no to ideas that look like they could go viral but would bring the wrong audience

### Mistakes to avoid

- making a max-ring video just because it could go viral
- switching topics every post, so the algorithm mixes your audiences
- chasing views at any cost and calling it a plan

### Where creators disagree

Some creators say to cover every problem your ideal customer has, not one narrow topic. The rings model keeps the same buyer too, but warns against the broad max ring. They differ on how far from the core to go

### Try it

Write the four idea rings for one topic you teach. Then check which ring your last three posts fell into

## 5.17 Doing more of what works, and testing new things

*9 min read*

> **The big idea:** A brand grows on what is proven and finds its next step in what is new. Use the 70/20/10 rule: 70% proven content, 20% small changes to what works, 10% brand new experiments. And when something wins, make more of it

### The 70/20/10 rule

70% of your content is what you know works. Your audience likes it, and you have enough data to say it will keep working

20% improves that 70% with small changes, like a new hook, a different format or a different structure. Nothing wild

10% is experiments: trying something entirely new. Most teams skip this, but it is the only way to find new formats and packaging

The 70% keeps your brand growing today, and the 10% is how you find what comes next

### How to run experiments

Don't do "one experimental post out of every 10". Out of every 30 days, set aside 3 days for experiments

Doing them in one batch saves time, and you come up with 2 or 3 ideas at once

Experiments are allowed to flop. The more experiments you run, the closer you get to a win

Keep tracking your data. If you get 7 days in a row of poor results, look into why

To decide between two versions, run an A/B test, which means showing both and seeing which does better. Let the audience decide instead of arguing about taste

### Do more of what wins

A common failure is people who grow a little but don't make more of their best ideas. They forget what made them grow

Each month, look at your best content and save the links. Then rewrite each piece from different angles: some short, some long, some lists, with a better hook or a clearer main problem

When a post does very well, make 5 more versions. Don't think "I already did that one"

Top creators repackage their winners instead of chasing new ideas

Expect this to take 6 months to a year before it pays off

### Stay open to change

Every format, angle and package gets old. The first person to try something new gets noticed, and most creators only copy second or third

Keep most posts on formats you know work, and set aside creative time to plan new angles

### Mistakes to avoid

- only doing the 70% and never testing anything new
- forcing one new thing a week instead of doing experiments in one batch
- celebrating 7 days of flops instead of looking into them
- growing a little and forgetting what made you grow

### Where creators disagree

Some creators give a number, like 10% experiments or 3 days out of 30. Others say to focus on a few formats and make more of them

They agree that you need both proven content and experiments

### Try it

Sort your last 10 posts into three piles: proven, improved and new. Check whether the split roughly matches 70/20/10

## 5.18 Making things worth spreading

*8 min read*

> **The big idea:** Ask what you can create that people want to react to, use, test or share. That works better than asking who can promote your post

### Give people something to quote

Plan how your post will spread by asking two questions: who would share it, and what would they say about it?

"Excited to announce our new platform" gives people nothing to talk about. Show the concrete thing instead

Say you turned a messy inventory spreadsheet into a working app. Show what you started with, what the tool made, and what you had to fix yourself. Now a business owner recognises their own problem, and someone tries it on their own spreadsheet

Test before you publish: what could a person who doesn't know you add to this post? If that is hard to answer, add something more specific

### Create things worth spreading

Ideas that work well:

- a benchmark: a comparison with a clear scoreboard
- a dataset: a collection of data
- a teardown: taking something apart to show how it works
- a strong opinion
- a public experiment, or a product to try

Let people test your workflow, compare against your benchmark and add their own result. The more it means to them personally, the less you have to push it

### Find the angle for a product

The biggest beginner mistake with a first product is not looking for the right angle first. The angle is how you present the product so people see it as valuable. You wouldn't call it "Writing Course". You'd call it something that makes people want it

Social media shows you what people want, through what they like, reply to and share. Say a video called "how to focus for 12 hours straight" goes viral. A product called "How to study for 12 hours straight" will likely sell well

You can also use those viral posts as ideas for your own posts, with your product behind them

### Use new tools early

When a new AI tool comes out, make something interesting with it, because the first people to show what it can do get attention

Treat every project as a test for the future, not as a picture of how things are now, because quality only goes up

### Mistakes to avoid

- posting an announcement that gives people nothing to talk about
- chasing disagreement without thinking about what people end up believing
- naming a first product plainly, like "Writing Course"
- treating every project as a picture of today when the tools keep getting better

### Try it

Take one post you plan to write and ask what a stranger could add to it. If you can't answer, add something more specific

## 5.19 One big video instead of 10 normal ones, and the 3 stages of packaging

*8 min read*

> **The big idea:** A plan for a viral video in any niche: put the effort of 10 normal videos into one big idea, package it so people stop scrolling, get curious and click, then repeat it enough times to give luck a chance

### Normal videos and big videos

Most channels are built on normal videos: normal ideas and normal effort, fine but unlikely to get big

The algorithm rewards big results far more than small ones. You can make 10 normal videos, or you can put the effort of those 10 into one video. That one is your big video

A much better idea with much better work behind it can get many times more views, not just a little more

### What makes a big video

- a broader idea that everyone in your niche could click and enjoy, beginners and experts, big fans and casual ones
- more time than feels reasonable, like two weeks to film
- more effort in every step. If you usually make 2 thumbnails, make 10

It may not make money by itself. The goal is a video that helps the whole channel, because reaching a new audience keeps paying off

Not every video should be a big video. Go bigger once a month or once a quarter

### The three stages of packaging

Viewers decide in a fraction of a second, in three stages:

1. Stopping the scroll: something striking, like a picture, an emotion or a few words, makes them stop. Usually the thumbnail does this
2. Interest: a curiosity gap opens a question in their head, like "wait, how is a flip-book that small possible?"
3. The click: they decide whether to act, depending on the value you promise and whether they believe you

Check your own packaging with these: what stops the scroll, what opens the question, and what makes people click?

### Give luck more chances

Going viral is about creating more chances for luck. One try is not enough, so try 5 videos, 10 videos, 50 videos

The full plan in order: a big video idea, packaging that passes the three stages, then repeat

**Example: The tiny flip-book**

Say a video shows a flip-book so small it looks impossible. It stops the scroll with the picture, opens the question "how is that possible?", and gets the click because the viewer believes the video will show them

*Why it works:* Each stage has one job, done in under a second

### Mistakes to avoid

- going for a big video on every video
- never going for a big hit at all
- packaging that needs 5 seconds to understand
- judging one try instead of 10 or 50

### Try it

Take one idea you already planned and write how it would look as a big video: a broader topic, double the time, 10 thumbnail options. Then run its packaging through stopping the scroll, interest and click
